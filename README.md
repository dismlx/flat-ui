# Flat UI

Flat UI restyles the default interface in WoW: Forever with flat backgrounds and softly shaded bar fills, borderless by default or with thin dark borders. It has its own options window and profiles. It has handles to move and size frames and windows. It also draws combat numbers in front of enemy nameplates and in scroll areas beside your character. In your bags and bank it marks items that bind when equipped, tints items you cannot use red and marks the food a hunter's pet eats.

Every setting starts at the settings of **Jon Default**, the author's own profile, and a new install and every new profile start with its frame positions, fitted to your screen at the scale the profile gives (a profile you have keeps its own).

A new character is welcomed with a picture of their own frame and a short setup that walks round the screen: your frame, your target and party, action bars, nameplates, chat and helpers, each with its choices drawn side by side. Nothing changes until you finish, and every setting stays in the options window. A fight hides the welcome screen, the ready-made looks and the card after Finish; chat says so, and they come back on the same view when the fight ends.

The options window lists its pages in eleven groups, by what they set: **Look**, **Frames and bars**, **Action bars**, **Combat** (your own fight), **Group**, **Character and gear** (**Gear comparison**, **Spell tooltips**, **Hit table** and **Trinket queue**), **HUD** (**Minimap**, **Buffs and debuffs**, **Quests and loot**, **Messages and timers**, **Experience and menu bars** and **Data panels**), **Chat and windows**, **Bags and items**, **Helpers** (**Errands**, **Drink and health macros**, **Mount key** and **World and cursor**) and **Layout**. A page that holds part of a module, such as **Buffs and debuffs** of Minimap and HUD, shows that module's switch. The window fades in with the game's window sounds. Drag its title bar to move it; Ctrl and the mouse wheel over the title bar set **Window scale**, and pointing at the title bar says the scale now. The mouse wheel scrolls a page also over a slider; Shift and the mouse wheel step a slider, as does the wheel while its number box has the cursor. Out of a fight, Ctrl+F puts the cursor in Search, Ctrl+Z puts back the newest change on any page (the strip says what, as "Undid Border colour: back to #000000.", and each press goes one change further back in Home's **Recent changes**), Escape closes the colour picker (keeping the old colour), an open dropdown or question first and the window on a second press, and Enter applies the colour picker's colour or answers a question with its first button. Enter does not answer a question that loses something for good: **Delete**, **Reset**, **Copy** and **Replace** on the Profiles page wait for a click. In setup too, Escape closes an open dropdown, question or the colour picker before the window. Every sound dropdown, such as **Jump sound**, **Warning sound** or a page's **Sound**, plays a sound once as you pick it, and the **Play** button right of it plays the one chosen now (dimmed at None), on the options pages, in setup and in the tracker editor. A long dropdown, such as the fonts or textures, opens with the cursor in its filter field: type to narrow it, Enter picks the first line and Escape closes it. **Reset page** is dimmed while nothing on the tab is changed, and after a reset the strip says how many settings went back; Home's **Recent changes** can undo each.

On **Home**, a click on a line of **Recent changes** or **Needs a reload** opens that setting's tab, scrolls to its row and flashes it, as a search result does; the line lights up under the mouse. Each recent change says what it was, from the value before to the value now, as "Off to On" or "300 ms to 450 ms", and pointing at its **Undo** names the value it puts back. Search lights the words you typed in each result, says how many results it found under its title, and a **Profiles** result such as **Import profile** or **In a raid** opens the Profiles page at that row and flashes it. Search also finds the window's own actions, under **Actions**: **Test mode** (**Turn on** or **Turn off**), **Move frames**, **Welcome screen** and **Ready-made looks** (**Open**). The small reset arrow on a changed row names what it puts back, as "Reset to default: On", "Reset to default: 300 ms" or, for a colour, a swatch and its code. Each row of the **Profiles** page says what it does when you point at it. **Duplicate**, beside **Create** under **New profile**, copies the profile in use with its frame positions, under the name you type or as "<name> copy", and puts the copy in use, while Create starts from the defaults. The positions of a pasted profile are fitted to your screen and UI scale, as those a new profile starts with are, so a frame keeps its share of the screen and none lands off a smaller one. A profile made on a screen of another size shows **Fit positions to this screen** under the paste field, on; turn it off to keep the positions as the text has them. With **Frame positions** off no position is imported, so the Screen line and Fit positions go, and the count of a part left out is dimmed. **Delete profile** asks 'Delete "Raid" for good?' and says how many other characters use it, who start as new characters at their next login; **Reset profile** says that every setting and every frame position goes back. Picking a profile under **Switch automatically** for the kind of place you are in now asks "You are in a raid now. Switch to Raid now?", with **Switch now** and **Later**; otherwise the switch waits until you next enter such a place.

Most settings apply at once. When a change needs a reload, wherever you made it, a small notice at the top of the screen says what needs it, with **Reload** (which also works where the game blocks reloads for add-ons, outside a fight), **Undo** and **Later**, which hides it until your next such change; it goes by itself when you put the change back. Point at the notice or any of its buttons for every change with its value now and at login, such as "Square icons: Off now, On at login". **Undo** sets them all back to what the game runs since login, so no reload is needed, and chat says how many it put back; it is dimmed and waits in a fight and while Setup has the options window. The notice stays clear of the options window's title bar, the `/flatui move` toolbar and the test mode bar, just under them or above them. Move it with the **Reload notice** box in `/flatui move`; while test mode is on and nothing needs a reload, a dimmed sample notice ("Sample: 2 changes need a reload: Square icons, Side tabs.", with greyed buttons) shows at its spot, stepping clear of the same bars. Where the game blocks reloads for add-ons, every **Reload** button of Flat UI is dimmed in a fight and a press says "Flat UI: Reload works here again after the fight. Type /reload to reload now."; it comes back after the fight.

The **Errands** page, on by default, does the chores the game leaves to you: it sells grey items at merchants, and your sell list twelve at a time so the buyback still holds them, repairs with your gold or the guild's where you choose (one chat line says what was sold and repaired), buys a full stack or as many as your gold, the stock and your bags allow after a Shift-click and a question, can answer the merchant's questions before a purchase with tokens or the sale of tradeable loot, loots every slot at once, makes room in full bags with a button that names the grey item it deletes, learns everything at a trainer with one button that says how many it will learn, guards Release Spirit in dungeons, types DELETE for you, and can accept summons, resurrections and invites from friends or your guild, decline duels and keep the last mail recipient. The Release Spirit guard, DELETE, summons, resurrections, invites, duels and the mail recipient are on its **Popups and invites** tab. Its **Quests and levelling** tab accepts and hands in quests, marks the quest reward a merchant pays most for with a small gold coin on its icon as the window opens, picks gossip options, offers the new ranks of your spells for your action bars (on a line at the top of the screen that the **New spell ranks line** box of `/flatui move` places), shows the reputation you gain, reminds you of talent points, adds /way and turns on the game's in-game navigation, its marker over the quest or map pin you track, which Forever's options leave out.

The **Data panels** page, on by default with its two panels off until you show them, adds a top and a bottom panel with bag space, gold across your characters (with what you made or spent this session, and from ten minutes in what that is an hour, in its tooltip), durability (with how many items are broken in red, a tooltip that lists the lowest first with the repair cost, and no slot where gear does not wear), your hearthstone, coordinates, the time (local or realm, 12 or 24 hours, as the game's clock is set), the quests in your log (red when it is full, with those ready to hand in and their experience in the tooltip), your experience (the share of the level and the rested share in blue, with what is left, the quests ready to hand in and the time to level in its tooltip; nothing at the top level), your reputation (the faction Flat UI's reputation bar shows, else the one the game watches, with its standing in colour and the share done, and in its tooltip what is left to the next standing and the reputation pace), frame rate and latency, friends and guild online, and the equipment set you wear, which a click changes. A click on bag space opens or closes Flat UI's bags; a click on the time opens the stopwatch (Shift-click the calendar), and one on the quest log, friends, guild, durability or reputation opens the quest log, the friends list, the guild window, the character window or your reputations, in a fight as it ends, with a line in chat that says so; Shift-click on coordinates, experience or reputation puts the line in chat; and a right click on any of these slots opens the Data panels page. The slot under the mouse lights up faintly. Each panel can be wider than its slots and sit over the game's windows, its text can take an outline, a shadow and your class colour or your own, and it can fade in or out of combat, back to full under the mouse. Frame rate and latency on the panels and by the minimap turn yellow and red when poor, and Flat UI's reputation bar can show reputation per hour and the time to the next standing.

The **Threat list** page, on by default, lists your group by threat on the enemy you fight, highest first, in class colours; at 100% a member has aggro. Your own bar has a thin white outline, and with **Always show your bar** it takes the place of the last bar when you rank lower, your place before your name ("12. Jon"). As damage or healer, while you are not tanking and pass **Warn at** (90%), your bar pulses red and a sound plays once for that enemy, and once you hold its aggro your bar keeps a steady red wash (the sound plays then if it has not, so a sudden pull is heard too); as a tank (your group role, else your stance or form) **Warn when you lose aggro** pulses it red and plays the sound once when an enemy you held turns to someone else (neither on bosses, where the game hides threat). The title shows the enemy's raid marker, such as a Skull, before its name, so you can tell which of three Defias Pillagers the list is about. The **Healer mana** page, on by default, shows your healers' mana in dungeons and raids; a bar turns red below **Low mana at** (30%), also in a fight, out of combat a cup and Drinking show who sits down to drink, and with **Dim healers out of range** the bar of a healer too far from you fades. Out of combat, while the game shows the numbers, its title reads the average mana of your living healers ("Healer mana 64%"), red while under **Low mana at**. Both windows fade in and out instead of popping, and their bars glide to each new value; a bar that turns to another member, or a new fight, takes its value at once.

The **Bags** page, on by default, puts all your bags in one Flat UI window, in sections (new items, equipment sets, equipment, consumables, quest items, trade goods, reagents, recipes, ammunition, bags and quivers, keys, other, junk and free slots) or bag by bag, each bag under its icon and name (bags with the same name add their number, such as bag 3), with a search that dims what does not match and shows in its box how many items match (red at none), your gold, whose tooltip says what you made or spent this session, and your free slots. Next to your gold the footer shows the currencies you tick **Show on Backpack** on the Currency tab of the Character window, each with its icon, its count and the game's tooltip (another character's as Flat UI last saw them), and a click on one opens the Currency tab (**Currencies in the footer**). Right-click the search box for quick searches (new items, junk, bind on equip, quest items, what you cannot use, recipes you can learn, consumables, trade goods, green or epic and better) and, under Recent, your last five searches, kept between sessions; tip:stamina or tip:"fire resistance" looks inside each item's tooltip, and combines with the rest (tip:agility #boe). A recipe you can learn now has a small green plus at its bottom left (**Item marks**), and #learnable lights them all. A key under Flat UI in Key Bindings opens the bags with the search box ready to type in, and another (**Flat UI bags: clean up**) cleans up your bags with the game's sound, as the sort button does. Nothing moves while the window is open: what you loot gathers at the top under New items, and anything else that comes into your bags meanwhile under Added. Drop an item on a section's title to keep it there, or on Junk, which is always there to drop on, to sell it at merchants from then on. Drag a section's title to put the sections in your own order, or right-click it to move it up or down. Click a section's title to fold it to one line, in both windows, and again to open it (the window keeps its height meanwhile, so the title stays under your mouse); Shift-click it to open only that section, every other folded (Shift-click again to open them all), and its right-click menu folds every section at once. Point at a folded title to see what it holds, each item with its count; while you search, a folded title says how many of its items match, and **Folded sections** opens them again. Ctrl+right-click an item to pin its slot to it (or drop it on Keep in place, if Ctrl is your Auto Loot Key): it shows in Keep in place, in the order of your bags and never sorted, and Flat UI puts it back in its slot after a clean up and when it comes back to your bags. Loot the game puts in an empty pinned slot while the window is open shows in that slot's place in Keep in place until the window opens again. A Flat UI bank window works at the banker, with Deposit and Take out for what you searched, or a whole section from its title's right-click menu (both only while **Deposit and Take out** is on), and shows your bank anywhere else as you last left it; pointing at a bank bag slot there lights that bag's items and says how full it is. Each bank bag shows its free slots in its corner, as the bag slots do, and an empty bought slot shows the game's faded bag art; click a bank bag to show only that bag, its edge lit, until you click it again, and drag it to pick it up. A row of your bag slots over the bag window's footer (**Bag slots**), with your keyring at its end while the game shows one, under a thin line apart from your items, shows each bag's free slots in its corner, gold at 4 or fewer and red when it is full (and the keyring how many keys it holds), puts a bag in or swaps one, also while the game's bag bar is hidden, and pointing at a bag there or on the game's bag bar lights its items in the window (an empty slot lights nothing); the tooltip opens beside the window, clear of the items it lights. While a spare bag sits in your bags, each empty slot it fits stays lit, and a click there puts it in, largest first. Drop a key on the keyring, or click it with a key on your cursor, to put it on your keyring. Click a bag there to show only that bag, its edge lit, until you click it again; drag it to pick it up. Right-click it, in either view, for that bag's settings (Assign To, and Ignore for Clean Up and Junk Selling, also on a bag's label in Bag by bag), which its tooltip names. A slot glows while the bag on your cursor fits it, greys while its bag is lifted, and loot pops on the slot of the bag it goes into. Pick another of your characters in a window's title to see their bags and bank, and an item's tooltip names who else on your realms carries it. A Sell junk button at merchants sells what Errands would sell, also with Errands off; its tooltip, and the Junk title's in any window, say what the junk is worth. The game's bag windows stay hidden while it is on; it stays off while another bag addon is loaded.

The **Drink and health macros** page, on by default, keeps two macros up to date with your bags and level: one drinks your best drink, conjured water first when it is as good, and in a fight takes a mage's mana gem, then a mana potion, and the other takes a healthstone, then a healing potion, and eats out of combat. With **Say when a macro changes** on, chat says when a macro moves to a better drink, food, potion or healthstone, or has none left in your bags ("Flat UI: Flat UI Drink now drinks Morning Glory Dew."), but not at login.

The **Mount key** page, under **Helpers**, off by default, gives you one key that mounts a random mount you can ride, favourites first; a druid takes Aquatic or Travel Form and a shaman Ghost Wolf where a mount cannot go, and pressed again it dismounts or leaves the form. With **Slow Fall or Levitate while falling** on, a mage or priest who presses it while falling, out of a fight and not mounted, casts that spell on themselves instead. Set the key on the page with **Key**: pick **Press a key to set it**, press the key (with Shift, Ctrl or Alt if you like) and chat names it, with what the key did before, which comes back when Mount key is off or you pick **Clear Flat UI's key**; Escape cancels. A key bound to Mount or travel form under Flat UI in the game's Key Bindings works as well.

The **Trackers** can count who in your group lacks your class buff (2/5 on its icon), and out of combat a click casts it on them; it also casts your own buff, stance or form, and calls, revives or sets your pet to fight. **Click to apply** can also put poisons, stones or oils on your weapon, or cast a shaman's weapon imbue. A pet tracker shows your pet's health in red while it is low. Trackers can warn minutes ahead of a long buff or poison, earlier in dungeons and raids, and play a sound when a buff comes back or gains a stack, or with **Sound when it turns low** (off by default, and per tracker) as a tracker turns low. With the mouse over a tracker, **Tracker tooltips** (out of combat only by default) shows its name, whether it is up, low or missing in green, amber or red, its time left and charges, a dim line with what counts as low ("Low at 2 charges or fewer or under 5:00 left", earlier in a dungeon or raid, "Low under 5 in your bags", "Low at 200 or fewer"), and for a group buff who lacks it, in class colours, while a click still goes through to the world; a tracker you cannot see, such as a proc that is down, has no tooltip and lets the mouse through. A tracker set to show only when low or missing fades in and out, and **Row direction** can also stack the trackers in a column, downward or upward, left-aligned as timer bars usually are. With **Close up the gaps** (off by default) a hidden tracker takes no room, so the ones that show sit together, centred or from the start of the row or column as **Row direction** says; in a fight nothing moves, and a tracker that shows there takes a place after them. A low tracker is amber, its rim, count and bar, also on the game's own icon in fights and as the border of **Mark action buttons**, and a missing one red, so "renew soon" reads apart from "gone". **Hide while mounted or flying** (on by default) also hides the trackers set to show only when low or missing on a flight path, until you land. The large proc art of **Proc alerts** has a **Proc art** box in `/flatui move` to put it anywhere, in the middle of the screen until you move it; **Art height** still raises or lowers it from there.

The **Group tools** page, on by default, adds a row of buttons for your group: the eight raid markers and a clear for your target, a ready check, a role poll and a pull countdown. During a ready check the tick shows how many are ready (3/5, green when all are, amber when someone is not) and its tooltip names who is not ready or has not answered, for 8 seconds after the check; during a countdown the pull button counts the seconds left. With **Borders in the marker's colour** each marker's border takes its colour (star yellow, circle orange, diamond purple, triangle green, moon silver-blue, square blue, cross red, skull white), and the tooltips of the markers and the clear name the game's own marker keys where you bound them. In test mode the tick shows a sample tally (4/5, a sample Eli not ready) and the pull button counts down from **Countdown length**, again and again. With **Fade until you point at it** (off by default) the buttons stay faint (30%) until the mouse comes over one, back to full at once and faint again a second after it leaves; they still take clicks, and stay full during a ready check or a countdown, in a fight too.

The **Ping bar** page, under **Group**, on by default, adds a bar with a button for each ping of the game's ping wheel, with the game's own icons: **Ping** (the game picks the kind), **Attack**, **Warning**, **On My Way** and **Assist**. Click a button, then click anywhere on the screen to ping that spot for your group, in a fight too: the cursor turns into the game's ping cursor, the button lights up in its ping's colour and a line by the bar names the ping that waits; click another button to switch to it, the same one again, right-click or Esc to cancel. A ping that waits gives up after 10 seconds out of combat, or as the fight ends if it waited through one, so a stray click never eats your next click on the world. Shift-click pings your target at once (with no target it works as a click). Each ping also has a key of its own under **Flat UI** in the game's Key Bindings (Not a threat and Threat too), which pings what is under the mouse at once, as the game's own ping keys do: hover the spot, press the key. Each button's tooltip says what a click and a Shift-click do and names its keys, and with **Keys on the buttons** each button shows its key, short as on action bars ("M4", "s-F"), in its top right corner, updated as soon as you rebind. The line that names the waiting ping hangs inward from the bar, so it stays on screen with the bar at an edge or laid out as a column. It shows in a group, always or never (keys only), hides while pings are off in the game's options or your group lets only some members ping, and shows a swipe while the game makes you wait before the next ping. `/flatui move` places it; **Button size**, **Spacing**, **Layout** (horizontal or vertical) and **Borders in the ping's colour** set its look, and **Fade until you point at it** (off by default) keeps the buttons faint until the mouse comes over one, full while a ping waits.

The **Absorb shields** page, under **Combat**, on by default, shows the shields on you as bars, in the manner of the NanShield display: the total of every absorb on you, and a bar for each Power Word: Shield, Ice Barrier, Mana Shield, Fire, Frost or Shadow Ward, Sacrifice, protection potion or item with its icon, the amount it has left, its time left and the colour of the school it takes. In a fight, while the game hides your buffs, the total keeps its live amount and each shield shows its time. A sound can play when a shield breaks, while the bars flash red, and picking a sound plays it once with the flash, so you see and hear the warning before a shield breaks; your target's total can have a bar too. With **Bar tooltips** (On by default) the mouse over a shield's bar shows the game's tooltip of its buff with what it has left, and over the total bar each shield on you with its amount and time left; a click still goes through to the world.

The **Defensives on you** page, under **Combat**, on by default, shows the shields, wards and protections other players cast on you, such as Pain Suppression, Blessing of Protection or Power Word: Shield, each with its time left and the caster's name in their class colour. **Buff tooltips** (on by default) shows the game's own tooltip over each icon, always, out of combat only or never, as on **Your buffs**, while a click still goes through to the world. Its samples in test mode and `/flatui test icons` follow **Icon border** and Borderless and carry the casters' names as the real icons do. **Sound when one lands** (None by default; Raid warning, Alarm or Whisper warning) plays once as a listed defensive from someone else comes on you, also in a fight, since the game plays it itself; spells you know yourself are left out, so a priest's own Power Word: Shield stays silent.

Each unit frame shows its portrait beside its name row and bars, the portrait as tall as the frame, with the portrait and each bar in a thin border of its own. The portrait can be a face, square or round, a 3D head, a class icon or none, and **One frame**, under **Frame style** on the **All frames: bars and pictures** tab of **Unit frames**, puts the whole frame on one background inside one border, its parts the same few pixels apart. The health bar of the frame under the mouse brightens a little (**Highlight under the mouse**, on by default), raid frames and the pet target frame too, in the bar's own rounded shape. With a **Health text** format chosen, the bar of a dead, released or offline unit reads Dead, Ghost or Offline, and the target of target and focus target have a **Health text** of their own (Hidden at first). The name rows of **All frames: text** (font, size, outline and colour) reach the names on the raid frames too, in exactly the colour the row names, also while the game's own class colours for raid names are on, and **Bar text font** and **Bar text outline** their status text.

On party and raid frames, a debuff you can dispel colours the border of the health bar, also on your own, your target's and your focus's frames, with its type icon on party frames. Party and raid frames show raid markers, raid frames the leader and assistants; a tank can keep a plain border while holding aggro, role icons can be limited to the roles you choose, and your target's border can take a colour of your own. With **Squares for your heals over time** (off by default), each heal over time of yours on a member, such as Renew, Rejuvenation or Regrowth, and a priest's Power Word: Shield (white) and Prayer of Mending (yellow, moving with the bounce) and a shaman's Earth Shield (orange), shows as a small square of its own colour in a corner of the health bar. Setup draws them: in the **Auras, flags and icons** step Mara carries a Magic debuff you can dispel, with the blue border inside her health bar and the Magic icon at its right end, and in the **Party frames** step Bex shows two heal over time squares in the corner and size you choose. **Sound for a debuff you can dispel** (None by default) plays a sound as a member gets one, at most once every 2 seconds, and plays it once as you pick it. **Low health border** (off by default, under **Status colours** on **All frames: bars and pictures**) turns the health bar border of your own frame and your pet's, your party's and their pets', and your raid's frames red, or a colour you pick, while the member is below **Low health below** (20% at first), also in a fight, and never on a dead member. With **Pulse the low health border** (off by default) the border beats softly while the member is below the limit and stops as they heal, every border on screen in one rhythm, and none on a frame the game hides; setup's and test mode's borders beat too. **Also on target, focus and boss frames** (off by default) gives your target's, focus's and boss frames' health bar the same border at the same limit, for Execute and Hammer of Wrath, and hides it on a dead unit. Setup's **Bars, heals and borders** step draws it: with the row on, Mara drops to 15% with the border round her health bar, gone again once the limit is under 15%, and with **Also on target, focus and boss frames** on the Defias Pillager drops to 15% with it too.

A druid in Bear or Cat Form sees their mana in a thin bar under the player frame, which fades in and shows the mana of the moment at once each time it appears, and fades out as they shift back to caster form; with the frame's **Power bar** off it hangs right under the health bar, in both Frame styles. **Pet target frame**, off by default on the **Pet frame** tab of **Unit frames**, shows what your pet attacks, with its name and its health in percent, in the name and bar text fonts of the unit frames, and its raid marker just left of it (with **Raid marker on target, focus, boss and pet target frames**); its bar and words fade in as it appears; a click targets it, a right-click opens its menu while it is your target too (set focus, raid marker) or a player, the mouse over it shows its tooltip and brightens its bar, its bar shows a new unit's health at once and glides to each later value, and it turns full and grey with Dead once the unit dies. With **Border while it is not your target** (on by default) its border turns **Not your target colour** (amber) while your pet attacks something other than your target, and goes back once you are both on the same unit, also in a fight. Its box in **/flatui move** is as wide as the frame at any **Pet target width**, and a wider frame grows to the right. Both bars take the **Corners** of the unit frames.

The **Cooldowns** icons turn a spell red while your target is out of its range and blue while you lack the power for it, can show your healing potions, mana potions and healthstones with how many you carry, any item with a use, the on-use gear you wear and how many reagents you carry for a spell, can take the spells of an action bar at once, show tooltips, and can give each icon a ready sound of its own. Up to three more rows, each off until you switch it on, take the icons you give them with **Row of one icon**, with a place, size and direction of their own; each row can show only in combat, out of it, in a group, in a dungeon or raid or when not mounted, and can hang under the player or target frame. One icon can show only on cooldown, only when ready or with a border while it cools down, and icons can glow when ready or on procs and show the time left of the buff they gave you. Their **Cooldown pulse** tab, on by default, fades the icon of a spell, trinket or item you used in the middle of the screen when its cooldown ends, with a sound of its own for each spell or item if you like, at up to three spots, each spell or item at the spot you give it. **Opacity** (70% by default) sets how solid a pulse is at its brightest, and an item's name under the icon shows in its quality's colour. Out of combat, changing **Icon size**, **How long it shows**, **Opacity**, **Sound**, **Name under the icon** or **Grow as it fades** shows a test pulse at each spot in use in the new look, and a **Sound** picked plays with it.

Nameplates show your threat on each enemy, on by default: the health bar shows threat you want, changing hands or unwanted, reading you as a tank from your group role, stance or form, and an enemy another tank or your pet holds has a colour of its own. On the **Threat** tab of **Nameplates**, a ring in the threat colour marks each enemy that needs you and flashes twice as an enemy turns to you, or away from you while you tank (**Flash the ring when threat goes wrong**, on by default, under **Glow and opacity**), enemies whose threat you want fade so the others stand out while your target and focus stay clear, easing to their new opacity over a fifth of a second as threat moves so the pack does not blink, and each enemy shows your share of its threat left of its health bar in a group or with a pet, past its raid marker and its elite or rare icon, moving out as a marker comes or goes and the number grows. **Trinket queue**, a page of its own under **Character and gear**, on by default, swaps your trinkets for you. Each trinket slot has its own list, best first. Tick **Top trinket slot** or **Bottom trinket slot** (or the **Auto queue** box in the window; both are off until you do) and, whenever the trinket you wear is on cooldown, Flat UI puts on the highest trinket of that list you carry that is ready, and goes back to a higher one as soon as it is ready again. A trinket with 30 seconds or less of its cooldown left counts as ready (**Ready with this much left**), since putting on a trinket with a use starts a 30 second cooldown on it anyway, and one just put on is never swapped for a lower one in that time. A trinket with no use can be in the list too, and is worn while nothing above it is ready. **Delay** keeps a trinket on that long after you use it, so its buff runs out first; **Stop** keeps it on while you wear it. The game changes no trinket in a fight, so a swap waits for its end, and also while you cast, are dead, hold an item on the cursor or your bags are busy. A trinket you put on yourself, or with an equipment set, stays until you use it or press **Queue now**, a slot you empty yourself stays empty until you put a trinket in it or press **Queue now**, and a trinket that would bind when worn is left for you to put on once. A trinket whose cooldown Flat UI cannot read stays on, and one the game will not let you wear is left out until you press **Queue now**. Open the window with `/flatui queue`, the small square right of each trinket slot on the Character window (its colour says whether that slot's queue is on, waits, is held or paused, or is off; right-click it to turn the slot's queue on or off, Shift-right-click to pause every swap, drop a trinket on it to add it) or **Trinket queue window** on the page. There, switch between **Top trinket** and **Bottom trinket**, click a trinket under **In your bags** to add it (Shift-click puts it first) or drop one on the window, move one with the arrows (Shift to the top or the bottom), click its delay for 5 seconds more (right-click for less, Shift-click for none), tick **Stop**, take it off with the cross, and click its icon to wear it now (a copy already bound to you first, so a spare that binds when worn stays unbound). **Pause all** stops every swap until you press it again. Each swap is said in chat (**Say each swap in chat**) with an optional **Sound on a swap**, and `/flatui status` says what each slot waits for. The lists, each slot's **Auto queue** and **Pause all** are kept for each character, whatever profile is in use, so a profile that switches by itself in a dungeon or a raid never turns the queue off. The window has a place of its own in `/flatui move`. The idea follows the trinket queue of TrinketMenu by Gello; none of its code is used.

The **Minimap** page gathers other addons' minimap buttons in a column beside the map or a row under it (**Addon buttons**), and with **Flat addon buttons**, on by default, gives each gathered button a square dark plate with a thin border in place of its round gold ring, with a light square when you point at it; a button you tick in **Leave in place**, or the row off, keeps its own art. The lines of text around the map answer the mouse, in a fight too, and their tooltips stay live as you walk: point at the coordinates for the zone and subzone, at the durability for each item you wear, at the frame rate for your home and world latency and Flat UI's memory, and at the time for your local and the realm's time and how long this session has run, "This session 1h 20m" (**Tooltips in combat** at **Hide every tooltip**, on the **Tooltips** page, hides them in a fight with every other tooltip). Shift-click the coordinates to put "Elwynn Forest 45.3, 62.1" in the chat box you have open, or in one Flat UI opens, never as a /cast line in a macro you are writing, and click them for the world map; click the durability for the character window; click the time for the game's stopwatch and alarm, Shift-click it for the calendar. In a fight the window waits: chat says once "Flat UI: the stopwatch opens when the fight ends." (or the map, or the character window), it opens as the fight ends, and the tooltip's hints say "after the fight". The frame rate line lets clicks through. **Text size** (100%, 70% to 160%) sizes the four lines at once, and the row of addon buttons under the map and the room under the zone name follow. While the game's own coordinates show under the map (the game's Options, Interface, Coordinates), Flat UI's **Coordinates** line stays hidden, so your place shows once; turn the game's off to have Flat UI's, with its tooltip and Shift-click.

Flat UI's experience and reputation bars, on the **Experience and menu bars** page, answer clicks with **Clicks on the bar**, on by default: Shift-click either bar to put your progress in the chat box, ready to send, as "Level 34: 12,345 / 27,000 (45%), 1h 20m to level" or "Stormwind: Honored 4,200 / 12,000 (35%)", and click the reputation bar to open your reputations, in a fight as the fight ends ("Flat UI: your reputations open when the fight ends."); the tooltip ends with a hint. Large numbers read as the game writes them, 12,150 / 27,000, in the bar text (**As the game has it**, **Value and percent**, **Amount to go**, and the reputation bar's "Stormwind 4,200 / 12,000"), the tooltip, the chat line and the setup picture. With **Animate gains**, on by default, the fill slides up to its new value over about half a second at each kill or hand-in, and the stretch you just gained lights up and fades; a new level, another faction or a loss sets the bar at once. At a new level the whole bar lights up once in soft gold and fades over about a second as the fill drops to its new value, beside the level up line in chat, and a new standing with the same faction (Friendly to Honored) flashes the bar in the new standing's colour. With **Level up line in chat**, on by default, each new level says in chat how long the level you finished took and your play this session: "Flat UI: level 35, after 1h 42m at level 34 (2h 10m of play this session)." The fill takes **Rested colour** while you are rested only while **Rested experience** is on, and is **Experience colour** otherwise. The tooltip's rested line reads "Rested: 8,000 (30% of the level)" in **Rested colour**, as the data panels' Experience slot shows it, and with **Rested share in the text**, on by default, **Bar text** at **Percent** or **Value and percent** ends with "+30%" in Rested colour while you are rested. The empty part of each bar is its background, in the **Background colour** and **Background opacity** of the Flat UI page, inside a thin border while **Bar border** is on, drawn with **Borderless** on too, so a bar at low experience still reads as a bar from end to end.

The **Fading** page, on by default, fades bars and frames while nothing needs them, and can show the target and focus frames, your buffs, the objective tracker and the game's damage meter only in combat, in a group, in or outside an instance or when not mounted. With Flat UI's action buttons, its **Main action bar** and **Action bar 2** to **8** rows fade Flat UI's own bars, also while **Leave the game's action bars alone** is on (on by default); with the game's buttons those rows wait for it to be off. Its **Minimap buttons** row fades other addons' minimap buttons with the game's. Its **Bag bar** row fades the backpack, the bag slots, the reagent bag and the keyring together, and the mouse over any of them shows them all, also while **Leave the game's action bars alone** is on; its **Micro menu** row does the same for the micro menu's buttons and the help ticket button. **Micro menu faded opacity** and **Bag bar faded opacity** give those two a faded opacity of their own in place of **Faded opacity**, down to 0%, where they are gone until the mouse is over them and still take clicks. Its **Bag windows** row fades your open bag and bank windows, Flat UI's or the game's: a window shows in full as it opens, under the mouse, while an item is on the cursor, a stack is being split or you type in its search box. Its **Experience bar** row, off by default, fades Flat UI's experience and reputation bar with **Faded opacity**, **Wait before fading** and **Fade time**, back to full under the mouse and as **Stay visible** says; while block 1 of the **Bar block** page holds the bar with **Fade the block** on, it fades with the block. The **Objective tracker** rule also fades Questie's tracker while Questie is loaded, and the tracker's **Opacity in combat** still comes first in a fight, with no flash to full after it. **Opacity in combat** under 100%, on the **Quests and loot** page, eases the objective tracker (or Questie's) down over about a quarter of a second as a fight starts and back up over about 0.2 seconds as it ends, in place of a jump each way; where the **Objective tracker** rule takes the tracker back after the fight, it eases on from the fight's opacity.

**Quest count**, on by default on the **Quests and loot** page, shows your quests and the most the log holds, as 12/20, at the right of the Quests heading of the objective tracker, red while the log is full. With **Warn as the log fills**, on by default, the count turns amber at one free slot, and when the log becomes full in play it gives a short red pulse and chat says once "Flat UI: your quest log is full (20/20)."; the first count after login or a reload never warns. Point at it for the tooltip of the **Quest log** data panel (the quests, "Your quest log is full.", those ready to hand in and their experience); a click opens the quest log, in a fight as the fight ends ("Flat UI: the quest log opens when the fight ends.").

With the [Questie](https://github.com/Questie/Questie) addon, **Questie's tracker**, on by default on the **Quests and loot** page, under **HUD**, gives Questie's quest tracker the look of the objective tracker: Flat UI's background and thin border round it with **Tracker background**, a flat band with a thin border behind its heading and each zone heading with **Tracker headers**, **Heading size** on the zone headings, a thin border and a cropped icon on its quest item buttons with **Tracker item buttons**, **Opacity in combat**, and, while the **Fonts** page is on, its interface font, outline and shadow on every line. Questie's own font sizes stay, since it lays its lines out by them, and Questie draws no bars to restyle. The **Objective tracker** rule of the **Fading** page fades Questie's tracker as it does the game's. Off, or without Questie, Questie keeps its own look; Questie's settings are never changed.

The **Bar block** page, off by default, lines action bars 3 and 4, action bar 2 and the main bar up in one block, in that order from the top, with one box in `/flatui move`, **Action bar block**, also while **Leave the game's action bars alone** is on, which then leaves only the bars in no block to the game. While the page is on, Flat UI's experience bar sits right under block 1, exactly as wide as the block, and follows it as its bars, their size, its direction or its box change; the block keeps its room, and the stance and pet bars stay above it (**Experience bar under the bar block**, on by default on the **Experience and menu bars** page). With that row off, or with the page off as it is by default, the experience bar has its own box in `/flatui move`, at the place a new profile starts with until you move it, and its own **Width** again. **One size and spacing for every bar**, on by default, gives the bars of the blocks one button size and row length and lays action bars 4 and 5 flat like the others, after a reload. Up to three blocks each have their own bars, in the order you set with **Bars** (move a bar up or down, or take it out) and **Add a bar** (the main bar and bars 2 to 8, each in one block only), or in `/flatui move`, where each bar of a block has a tab inside the block's box: drag the tab up or down and let go, and the bar takes that place in its block, as a line across the block shows while you drag; drop it on another block's box to put it in that block. Escape, or letting go outside every box, leaves the bars as they were. The two ways change the same order. Each block has its own stack direction, alignment and gap, and its own box in `/flatui move`; blocks 2 and 3 are off until you give them a bar, and start beside block 1. A bar of a block that the game's options have off is turned on there, at login and when you add it. **Fade the block** eases the bars down to **Faded opacity** over **Fade time** (300 ms by default) once the mouse has been off the block for **Wait before fading** (500 ms), so a mouse passing over them on its way across the screen makes them flicker no more, and brings them back to full at once, in a fight, under the mouse, with **Full opacity with a target** (off by default) while you have a target and, with **Full opacity as you cast** (off by default), while you cast and for **Wait before fading** after each spell or item you use, so a buff, a meal or an opening cast out of a fight shows its cooldown; with Flat UI's own action buttons it fades those, and the game's bars hidden under them stay hidden. The **Class and item bars** page hangs the Class spells, Buffs to apply and Consumables bars from block 1.

The numbers on action button cooldowns, on Flat UI's buttons and the game's and on the restyled pet and stance bars too, take **Cooldown number size**, **Cooldown number colour** and **Tenths below** (under that many seconds they show tenths, as 2.4) on the **Colours and cooldowns** page under **Action bars**, and each goes back to the game's look at once, with no reload. They show while the game's own **Show numbers on cooldowns** is on, on Flat UI's buttons as on the game's, and follow it at once as you turn it on or off. **Cooldown sweep**, **Own sweep opacity** and its **Sweep colour** (black by default) and **Sweep opacity** go for the pet and stance bars as well. **Edge line** (off by default) draws a bright line in **Edge colour** along the edge of the sweep on the main and extra bars, as on the cooldown icons. With **Global cooldown sweep** off (on by default) a button of Flat UI's that only waits for the global cooldown shows no sweep, so the bars stay calm between presses and only real cooldowns sweep. **Flash when ready** flashes in **Flash colour**, white by default, and only on a button that shows. Flat UI's buttons paint themselves as their slots change, so a power tick in a fight no longer repaints all 96 of them.

With Flat UI's buttons, **Page arrows on the main bar** (on by default, under **Buttons** on the **Action bars** page) puts the page number with an up and a down arrow left of the main bar, where the game has them: the number follows the page as you change it with Shift and the wheel or a key, and the arrows page the bar, in a fight too. They move, hide and fade with the main bar, and Edit Mode's **Hide bar scrolling** hides them too. **Aim highlight** (on by default, on the **Glows and animations** page) makes the button of a spell you place on the ground, such as Blizzard, Rain of Fire, Flamestrike, Hurricane or Volley, pulse softly in the cast bar's **Cast colour** while the cursor waits for its spot, until you place or cancel it. The cast fill, the interrupt flash, the aim highlight and **Flash when ready** light only buttons you can see, never a button past a bar's **Number of icons** in Edit Mode.

The **Hidden frames** page (on, with nothing ticked) takes frames of the game off the screen: tick one and it is gone, untick it and it is back as it was. It lists the game's HUD frames that `/flatui move` moves, in groups (action bars, unit frames, minimap, buffs and debuffs, bars and timers, messages and alerts, trackers and meters), and the parts of the minimap one by one: the zone name, the tracking, mail, calendar, clock, addon and zoom buttons. The chat, the loot window, the tooltip, the windows (Character, Spellbook, bags and the rest) and Flat UI's own frames are not on it. The pet frame, the totems and the class resource bars go with the player frame while Edit Mode keeps them under it, and their boxes in `/flatui move` say so. **Alerts** takes each toast away as it comes. A hidden frame stays hidden when the game shows it again, also after a reload, and each tick's tip says what goes with it and what still works, as the keys of a hidden action bar, or B and the bag keys without the bag bar. In `/flatui move`, Shift and right-click a box to hide its frame (on a box with no tick it only says so in chat), or press **Hide frame** in the inspector; the **Hidden** group at the top of the frame list shows a frame again with its eye. A change in a fight waits for its end. While **Leave the game's action bars alone** is on, the game's bars stay; the micro menu and the bag bar hide either way, and the keys such as C, P and L still open their windows. The main bar and the frames the game stacks above it, such as the cast bar, keep their places with the micro menu hidden. `/flatui move` still does not move the micro menu while that row is on, since the game hangs its main bar from it. **Fading** leaves a hidden frame alone: it comes back at its own opacity and fades again from there. `/flatui hide` opens the page.

The game's windows open clear of your unit frames: where the game would put a window's title over the player or target frame at the top of the screen, as it does with the Character window, the Spellbook, the map and Professions, the window opens just under them, with any window beside it at the same height (**Open windows under the unit frames**, on by default in the **Windows** section of **Movers**). A window you drag by its title stays where you put it, and a unit frame you moved lower down leaves the windows where the game puts them. Nothing of the game's window layout is changed: in a fight a window the game protects stays where the game opens it.

On the **Windows** page the Spellbook has dark flat pages with light text, as the other restyled windows: spell names in white, the lines under them in grey, headers and the page number in gold. **Spellbook parchment**, off by default in **Window art**, gives the game's parchment pages and their dark text back. The quest giver, gossip, book and letter pages and the quest log details are dark with light text too, by default: **Dark quest, gossip and book pages**, on in **Window art**, sets the game's own Quest Text Contrast to Dark for your account, and the game draws its dark page and light text itself. Loot rolls get Flat UI's look with **Loot rolls**, on by default on the **Quests and loot** page: a flat background and thin border, the item's icon cropped with a border in its quality colour, the name in that colour, a flat timer bar with a thin border, and **Need**, **Greed** and **Pass** in a row; off gives the game's look back. With **Seconds left on rolls**, on by default, the whole seconds left to roll count down at the right end just above the timer ("39", "38" ...), and under 10 seconds the number and the bar turn red; the number goes away as the roll ends or the Need animation plays, and the setup picture and test mode's sample rolls show it too. **Hide error messages**, on by default on the **Messages and timers** page, hides the kinds of red error messages you choose in the middle of the screen, with their sound and voice: not enough mana, rage or energy, out of range, facing the wrong way, not ready yet, no or an invalid target, not while moving, and, off by default, not in line of sight. **When**, under it, is **Always** by default; **Only in a fight** hides them only in combat and shows every message out of it, so you still learn why a cast on a mob or a mailbox failed. **Hide the zone title**, **Hide the subzone title** and **Hide the talking head**, off by default on the same page, take away the big zone name and the talking head with its voice. **Clear the damage meter**, off by default on the same page, clears the game's damage meter as you enter a dungeon or raid, or asks first. **Seconds on the timers**, on by default on the same page with **Breath and fatigue timers**, puts the time left ("1:12", "0:42") at the right end of the label box under the breath, fatigue and feign death bars, with the label still centred; while the bar drains the time turns red under 10 seconds, and it stays white while your breath comes back.

**Hit table under the player frame**, on the **Hit table** page, under **Character and gear**, shows under your player frame your chances against your target to miss, be dodged, parried or blocked, glance, crit and hit, worked out again as your gear, buffs, weapon skill, form and target change: a line for your swings (each hand while you dual wield), your special attacks, your ranged attacks and your spells, picked by your class and form, with the crit cap of your swings, yellow once your crit is past it; a target whose level shows as ?? counts as three levels above you. Your spells also get **Resist**, no part of the roll: the share of a spell's damage resisted on average, with your Spell Piercing. The enemy's own resistances are not known and count as none, so with piercing it goes below 0, in green, for the damage a creature takes more (WoW: Forever's Spell Vulnerability, half of what a point of resistance takes, a point below 0; against a player piercing does nothing below 0); and a creature above you resists about 2% more a level, Classic's rule, an estimate on WoW: Forever that piercing does not lower, which a spell that also slows or roots pays with its higher miss instead. The game gives only the piercing of every school, so piercing of one school, as Fire Piercing, counts in the gear comparison and a spell's Expected line and not here; `/flatui status` says what Resist counts. Where **Show** hides it without an enemy targeted or out of a fight, it fades in over 0.15 s and out over 0.35 s, and an enemy targeted again during the fade brings it back at once. The table hangs under the player frame, and under the pet frame, the totems and a cast bar Edit Mode locks there; `/flatui move` gives it a spot of its own, as every new profile has from Jon Default, and wherever it is it steps down under your pet frame, or test mode's sample of it, where it would run into it, and back up when the pet goes. **On enemy tooltips**, on the same page and also **For tanks and damage dealers** by default, adds your hit table to the tooltip of an enemy you can attack: a line for each attack, as in "Swing  M8 D6.5 G40 C12.1 H33.4" (M miss, D dodge, P parry, G glance, B block, C crit, H hit, in percent; R on the Spells line, its Resist, as in R-2.5, with a dim note of what it counts while it is not 0), against that enemy's level (a level shown as ??, or a boss of your level or above, counts as three levels above you), with parries and blocks only from the front (a tank counts in front), following **Attacks**, **Special attacks**, **Where you stand** and **Hit cap against a boss** whatever **Hit table under the player frame** is set to; those rows stay open while it is **Off**. In a fight the game hides your stats, so the numbers there are from before it, and a dim line says so. With **Only while Ctrl is held**, under it and on by default, the lines show only with Ctrl: held as the tooltip shows, or pressing Ctrl adds the lines at once; they leave when the tooltip next changes, not as you let go (over a unit frame, whose tooltip the game sets again every 0.2 seconds, they go with the key). The tooltip is never built again for them, so it is otherwise as the game has it; **Hint while Ctrl is up** (off) puts one dim line, "Hold Ctrl for the hit table", in their place meanwhile, which becomes their header as you press Ctrl. It needs **Chat and tooltips** on. **Text size**, a dropdown on the same page, sets the size of the table's text, from 70% to 200%; a pick applies at once and is kept with your profile. **Text size on enemy tooltips** does the same for the hit table's lines on enemy tooltips alone: the tooltip's other lines, and every tooltip after it, keep the game's size.

The **World and cursor** page, under **Helpers**, can put a pulsing glow on the herb, vein or quest object your Interact Key would use, in gold or coloured by what it is (herbs green, ore copper, quest objects gold, loot white), fading in as it lands and, with **Glow sound**, ringing once out of a fight, with a row that turns on the game's Interact Key and its icons the glow needs in one pick, let the camera zoom out farther than the game's own options allow (ticking it glides the camera out to the new distance), and draw a ring around the mouse pointer, always or in combat only (fading in and out with the fight), with your cast running round it and, if you like, the global cooldown; the ring hides while you turn the camera with the mouse, and with **Grow when you shake the mouse** a quick shake swells it for a moment so you find the pointer. Picking a **Glow sound** or a **Jump sound** for the lava tick timer plays it. The unit frames can hide the hit numbers the game flashes over your portrait and your pet's.

**Lava tick timer**, on the same page and **In lava zones** by default, helps you walk or swim through lava. Lava burns you every 2 seconds, and a jump timed so you are in the air as the tick lands takes no damage, even when you land back in it. After a fire hit on you in Molten Core, Blackrock Depths or Spire, Ragefire Chasm, Searing Gorge, Burning Steppes or Un'Goro Crater (or anywhere, with **Anywhere**), a bar under your character counts down to the next tick and turns green with **Jump** for the last **Jump lead** (400 ms by default), with a flash and, if you pick one, a **Jump sound**. Each tick says **Hit**, and a tick you were in the air for says **Dodged**, then **Dodged x2**, **x3** as the dodges in a row build (a hit starts again; `/flatui lava` names your best this session); the bar goes 5 seconds after the last one. It is an estimate: the game tells add-ons that fire hit you, not that it was lava, so in a fight it counts only fire hits that keep the 2-second rhythm, and the first tick of each visit always lands. `/flatui move` places it, `/flatui lava` says what it sees, `/flatui lava test` shows a sample and `/flatui lava trace` prints each hit on you. The idea is the timing bar of the Classic WeakAura "Avoid lava damage"; none of its code is used.

## Install

1. Download the newest file from [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/projects/1750813) or from the releases of this repository.
2. Put the `FlatUI` folder in the `Interface\AddOns` folder of your WoW: Forever install.
3. Start the game and type `/flatui`.

## Commands

- `/flatui` opens the settings window.
- `/flatui setup` walks round your screen in six short stops and saves what you pick.
- `/flatui profiles` shows the ready-made looks. A click puts one in use, and **Back to** on the card that follows puts the profile you had back in use.
- `/flatui move` shows a box for every frame. Drag a box to move its frame; Shift and right-click the box of a HUD frame of the game to hide it. A name too long for its box is cut short with "...", and a box too small for one has none; point at a box to see it drawn over the others with its whole name.
- `/flatui hide` opens the Hidden frames page, where a tick hides a frame of the game.
- `/flatui status` says what is styled, and any error.
- `/flatui sell <item>` always sells an item at merchants (an item link or ID). Type it again to stop. `/flatui sell` alone lists the items. **Items always sold** on the Errands page takes one off.
- `/flatui keep <item>` never sells a grey item, and Make room never deletes it. **Items never sold** takes one off.
- `/flatui train` learns everything you can pay for at an open trainer, like the Train all button.
- `/flatui npc` skips the NPC you talk to or target: its quests and gossip are left to you. Type it again to stop.
- `/flatui gossip` lists the options of the NPC you talk to. `/flatui gossip 2` picks the second option for you from now on.
- `/flatui uprank` offers the new ranks of your spells for your action bars.
- `/flatui rank <spell>` keeps that rank where it is on your bars (a spell link or ID).
- `/way 45 61` puts a pin on your map and the game points you to it; `/way Elwynn Forest 45 61` for another zone, `/way clear` to take it off. `/flatui way` does the same when another addon has `/way`.
- `/flatui xp` says your experience per hour, time to level and kills and quests to level, and your reputation per hour and time to the next standing with the faction on Flat UI's bar. `/flatui xp reset` starts counting again.
- `/flatui threat` shows sample bars of the threat list for a few seconds, out of combat; `/flatui healers` does the same for the healer mana list.
- `/flatui lava` says what the lava tick timer sees: the place and its IDs, the ticks seen and dodged, the last hit on you and the gaps between ticks. `/flatui lava test` shows a sample for 8 seconds, and `/flatui lava trace` prints a line for each hit on you, to check in lava that the game reports its ticks.
- `/flatui cooldown add healthstone` puts your healthstones on the cooldown row, every rank as one icon; `healing` and `mana` do the same for potions, and `/flatui cooldown remove` takes one off.
- `/flatui cooldown sound <spell> bell` gives one icon of the cooldown row its own ready sound; `row` gives it back the row's.
- `/flatui cooldown add item <item>` puts an item with a use, such as the Hearthstone, on the cooldown row (an ID, a link or a name); `remove item` takes it off. `/flatui cooldown import 1` adds the spells with a cooldown on action bar 1 (2 to 8 likewise).
- `/flatui pulse test` shows a cooldown pulse; `/flatui pulse ignore <spell or item>` leaves one out and `/flatui pulse watch` puts it back; `/flatui pulse list` says what pulses.
- `/flatui pulse sound <spell or item> bell` gives one its own pulse sound; `none` silences it, and `page` gives it the page's sound again.
- `/flatui pulse spot <spell or item> 2` sends one to pulse spot 2; `1` puts it back.
- `/flatui role` says whether nameplate threat reads you as a tank and why; `tank`, `damage`, `auto` or `toggle` sets it.
- `/flatui gear` opens or closes the Gear comparison window; `/flatui gear <item>` compares a shift-clicked item there. The window's **Find** button opens the item finder. `/flatui gear add <spell>` (an ID, a name or a shift-clicked spell, which keeps its rank), `remove <spell>`, `bar 1` and `reset` change the spells it compares, `weight <spell> <0 to 5>` sets how much a spell counts in the **Overall** line, and `list` prints them.
- `/flatui queue` opens or closes the Trinket queue window (`/flatui trinkets` too); `/flatui queue top` or `bottom` opens it on that slot's list, `/flatui queue pause` pauses or resumes every swap, `/flatui queue now` lets the queue act at once on a slot you changed by hand, and `/flatui queue list` prints both lists.
- `/flatui tracker add group` tracks your class buff on your group; `/flatui tracker use 2 <item>` sets what weapon tracker 2 applies; `/flatui tracker set 1 health 40` shows pet tracker 1's health in red under 40%.
- `/flatui bags` opens or closes the Flat UI bag window; `/flatui bags <name>` shows another of your characters' bags.
- `/flatui bank` opens or closes your bank window: live at the banker with Flat UI's bank window, else as you last left it; `/flatui bank <name>` shows another character's bank.
- `/flatui test` turns test mode on or off (`/flatui test on` and `/flatui test off` also work); see **Test mode** below.
- `/flatui test icons` shows three sample icons for 8 seconds on Defensives on you, Your buffs, Party cooldowns and Enemy cooldowns.
- `/flatui help` lists the commands.

## Test mode

Test mode shows your frames on screen with sample content while you change options, so a setting can be seen as you change it, the frames that are hidden until a fight, a target or a group included. Turn it on with the **Test mode** switch on the title bar of the options window, left of Close; **Samples** on the toolbar of `/flatui move`; `/flatui test`; or a key (Key Bindings > Flat UI > **Flat UI test mode**). It does not turn on in a fight or while Setup has the options window.

A bar at the top of the screen (under the `/flatui move` toolbar while that shows, and just above or under the options window where it would cover that window's title bar; drag it to put it elsewhere until a reload) has **Group** (**Solo**, **Party**, **Raid 10**, **Raid 20** or **Raid 40**; Party at first, kept until a reload), **Moving** (on; off holds every sample still so a look can be judged), **Bags**, **Bank**, **Gear**, **Finder** and **Queue**, which open Flat UI's own windows with their real content, **Move**, which opens `/flatui move` with the samples kept on so each frame can be placed where it shows, and **End**. A double-click on the bar's title puts a dragged bar back at its own place. Each control lights up under the mouse and has a one-line tooltip that says what it does. A dim line under it names any frame that has no place yet ("open Edit Mode once"): a frame the game has never placed gets no sample at a corner.

Every change shows at once; a frame moved or scaled in `/flatui move` or Edit Mode takes its sample with it within a quarter of a second. The samples stay under the options window and the welcome screen: drag the window over them and its rows stay readable, with no combat alert, cast bar or cooldown pulse showing through it. A sample steps aside as soon as the real frame shows: a real target, cast, group or aura. Esc > Edit Mode still shows the game's own previews, and the samples give way to them. A frame ticked on the **Hidden frames** page gets no sample, and neither does a frame that hangs from it (the pet frame, the totems and a cast bar locked to the player frame).

It ends when you press the switch, **Samples**, `/flatui test`, the key or **End** again; when you close the options window it was turned on from (not while `/flatui move` holds that window); when `/flatui move` closes for good after turning it on (unless the options window is open, which then keeps it); when Setup takes the options window; and at a reload. A fight pauses it, and it comes back after the fight while what turned it on is still open. It saves nothing: no setting, game setting, key binding, target or focus, and no number counts in a statistic.

What does not show, and why:

- **The game's own unit frames are never shown or given a unit**, and **Edit Mode is never opened by Flat UI**: the game protects them, and doing either from an addon taints them and can block targeting. The samples are Flat UI's own frames drawn where the game's are, so a sample can differ slightly from the real frame.
- **Real nameplates are not forced on screen** (that would change game settings), so threat colours show on the sample plate, not on your target's plate.
- **The game's windows** (Character, Spellbook, talents and the rest) are not opened: an addon opening them can taint the game's window manager. Open them yourself and the **Windows** page restyles them as you change it.
- **No drawing exists** for mirror timers, the loot window, the queue panel, alerts, zone text, the talking head, the extra action button, arena and vehicle frames, and Errands' overlays need the NPC's window open. Chat and tooltips get no sample: a printed line would skip the chat filters, and a tooltip shows on any mouse-over anyway.
- **Party cooldown rows hang in the raid stack**, not beside the party samples, and the **pet's target** frame (a secure button of Flat UI's) shows no sample.

## Test

`FlatUI_test.lua` runs the addon in a simulated client. It needs Lua 5.4.

```
lua5.4 FlatUI_test.lua FlatUI
```

The last line must say `N passed, 0 failed`.

## Code map

`tools/dev/flatui_map.py` builds the code map of Flat UI as one page: each file with its functions, its settings, its calls to other files, and the game's frames, functions, events and CVars it uses. It needs Python 3, Lua 5.4 and git.

```
python3 tools/dev/flatui_map.py --fetch
```

`--fetch` clones Forever's interface source and the client lists of [wowless](https://github.com/wowless/wowless), which name the game's functions, into `tools/dev/sources`, or brings them up to date. The page is `tools/dev/out/flat-ui-map.html`, with its data beside it.

## Spell data

`FlatUI/SpellData.lua` holds the spell values of the spell lines and the gear comparison. `tools/dev/make_spelltips.py` makes it from the inputs in `tools/dev/spelltips`. After a client build or a hotfix, read the client's spell rows again from a new capture of wowsims/forever (`assets/db_inputs/spell_store_inputs.json`, with GrunzForever's export for the few rows it lacks), then make the file:

```
python3 tools/dev/spelltips_import.py spell_store_inputs.json.gz wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite
python3 tools/dev/make_spelltips.py
```

It needs Python 3. `make_spelltips.py --check` writes nothing; it lists every value of the inputs the new rows contradict.

## Rotation data

`FlatUI/RotationData.lua` holds the WoWSims rotations the gear comparison's **Overall** line counts: each ability's uses a minute and its share of the damage and threat, for each class and spec, and the share of the main hand's swings Heroic Strike and Maul take. `tools/dev/make_rotations.py` makes it from `tools/dev/rotations/data.json`, the results of the WoWSims Classic simulator's command line run with each spec's presets, and `FlatUI/SpellData.lua`, whose keys it matches the abilities to:

```
python3 tools/dev/make_rotations.py
```

It needs Python 3. `--check` writes nothing; it says whether the file is what the data makes.

## Item index

`FlatUI/ItemIndex.lua` holds the armor and weapons the item finder searches: each item's ID, English name, class and subclass, required level, item level, quality and the classes that may wear it, one string a slot, read only when the finder first searches that slot. `tools/dev/make_itemindex.py` makes it from GrunzForever's export of the client tables (`wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite`), leaving out the client's test and placeholder items:

```
python3 tools/dev/make_itemindex.py wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite
```

It needs Python 3. `--check` writes nothing; it says whether the file is what the export makes.
