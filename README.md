# Flat UI

Flat UI restyles the default interface in WoW: Forever with flat backgrounds and softly shaded bar fills, borderless by default or with thin dark borders. It has its own options window and profiles. It has handles to move and size frames and windows. It also draws combat numbers in front of enemy nameplates and in scroll areas beside your character. In your bags and bank it marks items that bind when equipped, tints items you cannot use red and marks the food a hunter's pet eats.

Every setting starts at the settings of **Jon Default**, the author's own profile, and a new install and every new profile start with its frame positions, fitted to your screen at the scale the profile gives (a profile you have keeps its own).

A new character is welcomed with a picture of their own frame and a short setup that walks round the screen: your frame, your target and party, action bars, nameplates, chat and helpers, each with its choices drawn side by side. Nothing changes until you finish, and every setting stays in the options window. The card after **Finish** ("Your screen is set") takes it back until you reload: after a save into the profile in use, **Undo setup** puts that profile back as it was ("Flat UI: Raid is back as it was before setup.", with "Game settings setup changed stay as they are." when it wrote some), as **Bring back** on the Profiles page does, and the Finish line in chat says so; after a Finish that made a new profile, **Back to Default** puts the profile you had back in use, as after a ready-made look. A fight hides the welcome screen, the ready-made looks and the card after Finish; chat says so, and they come back on the same view when the fight ends. The welcome screen and the card fade in with the game's window sounds, as the options window does; drag them by the title row while they are open (they open at the top of the screen again), and **Close** at the top right closes them without choosing anything, as Escape does.

The options window lists its pages in eleven groups, by what they set: **Look**, **Frames and bars**, **Action bars**, **Combat** (your own fight), **Group**, **Character and gear** (**Gear comparison**, **Spell tooltips**, **Hit table** and **Trinket queue**), **HUD** (**Minimap**, **Buffs and debuffs**, **Quests and loot**, **Messages and timers**, **Experience and menu bars** and **Data panels**), **Chat and windows**, **Bags and items**, **Helpers** (**Errands**, **Drink and health macros**, **Consumables bar**, **Mount key** and **World and cursor**) and **Layout**. A page that holds part of a module, such as **Buffs and debuffs** of Minimap and HUD, shows that module's switch. The window fades in with the game's window sounds. Drag its title bar to move it, or double-click the title bar to put the window back in the middle of the screen and forget its saved place ("The window is back in the middle."); Ctrl and the mouse wheel over the title bar set **Window scale**, and pointing at the title bar says so and the scale now. The title bar also names the profile your changes go into, as "Profile: Raid", dim, left of the **Test mode** switch on every page: point at it for how many characters use that profile, click it for the Profiles page, and it lights in the accent for a second when the profile changes while the window is open (a switch of **Switch automatically**, for one). A profile picked under **Profile in use** (on the Profiles page or Home's Profile card) or with `/flatui profile Raid` in a fight is kept: the strip (or chat, "Flat UI: switches to Raid when the fight ends.") says "Switches to Raid when the fight ends.", the title bar reads "Profile: Default" with "Raid after the fight" under it in the accent, and as the fight ends the profile switches and chat says "Flat UI: using Raid."; a switch of **Switch automatically** that waits for the fight's end shows on that line too, and pointing at it says which. Picking the profile in use again in the fight drops whatever waits, a rule's switch too ("Stays on Default."), and a pick wins over a switch of Switch automatically that waits for the same fight. The mouse wheel scrolls a page also over a slider; Shift and the mouse wheel step a slider, as does the wheel while its number box has the cursor. Out of a fight, Ctrl+F puts the cursor in Search, Ctrl+Z puts back the newest change on any page (the strip says what, as "Undid Border colour: back to #000000.", and each press goes one change further back in Home's **Recent changes**; after **Reset page** one press puts the whole page back, as "Undid Reset page on Cast bars > Size: 5 settings back."), Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z makes again what Ctrl+Z or an **Undo** took back, a whole Reset page in one press ("Redid Border colour: #FF3030 again.", or "Nothing to redo."), until you make a new change; either shows the row it changed when it is on the tab shown (its card opens, it scrolls into view and flashes in the accent, the first row of a Reset page), and names the place of one on another tab, as "Undid Border colour: back to #000000 (Unit frames > Bars and pictures).", Escape closes the colour picker (keeping the old colour), an open dropdown or question first and the window on a second press, and Enter applies the colour picker's colour or answers a question with its first button, which is in the accent. Enter does not answer a question that takes a profile's settings away: **Delete**, **Reset**, **Copy** and **Replace** on the Profiles page, and setup's **Start over**, are red, with "Enter does not answer this." under the question, and wait for a click. A question fades in, as the colour picker does, and its box grows with the question, so a long Delete or Copy from question is never cut off. In setup too, Escape closes an open dropdown, question or the colour picker before the window, and Enter applies the colour picker's colour or answers a question with its accent button (**Keep choices** when you close it). As with the game's own buttons, a press in the options window, setup, the welcome screen or on the test mode bar counts only when you let go over what you pressed, so sliding off a button before letting go changes your mind; a button's label sinks a pixel while you hold it. Every sound dropdown, such as **Jump sound**, **Warning sound** or a page's **Sound**, plays a sound once as you pick it, and the **Play** button right of it plays the one chosen now (dimmed at None), on the options pages, in setup and in the tracker editor. A right-click on any colour swatch opens a short list at it, **Copy #D93A30** and, once a colour is copied, **Paste #D93A30**: Paste writes it at once (it shows in Home's **Recent changes**, and Ctrl+Z puts the old colour back), and a row without its own opacity keeps its own; the strip says "Copied #D93A30. Right-click another colour to paste it.", the copied colour also leads the colour picker's recent colours, and pointing at a swatch says "Right-click: copy or paste." A long dropdown, such as the fonts or textures, opens with the cursor in its filter field: type to narrow it, Enter picks the first line and Escape closes it. **Reset page** is dimmed while nothing on the tab is changed, and after a reset the strip says how many settings went back and that Ctrl+Z puts them back; the strip's "3 changed on this tab. Click to go to each." lights under the mouse, and each click scrolls to the next changed row, opening its card, and flashes it, round to the first again after the last. A tab that holds a setting off its default has a small dot in the accent after its name (**Per frame** counts its eight frames), and a tab under **More** a dot after its line in the list; the dot goes once the last of them is back at its default. The sidebar does the same for each module: a dot after its name while any of its pages (its tabs and **Per frame**) holds a setting off its default, so you can see which of the 43 pages hold your own settings without opening them (none in the **Icon rail** layout, which shows no names). The **Search** box in the title bar has a small cross at its right while words are typed: a click clears them, shows the page you were on and keeps the cursor in the box for the next search. Home's **Recent changes** shows the reset as one line, "Reset page on Cast bars > Size" with "5 settings", whose **Undo** puts the whole page back. A message in the strip, such as what Ctrl+Z undid, fades in and holds for about six seconds whatever the mouse crosses, then the strip shows the count of changes on the tab again. Every switch, on the rows, in a page's header, on Home's tiles, the **Test mode** switch and **Moving** on the test mode bar, clicks with the game's own checkbox sound, higher for on and lower for off, and its knob slides across; a switch that changes by itself (a profile switch, Reset page, Ctrl+Z) slides with no sound. **Custom scale** is greyed and reads "not used now" while **Pixel-perfect scale** or **Scale below 65%** sets the scale, and **Small scale** while Pixel-perfect scale does; setup's **Scale** step shows **Scale below 65%** with the main rows, and the one of Custom scale and Small scale that sets the scale. A change of the UI scale or of the **Fonts** page made in a fight waits for the fight's end, and chat says so once a fight while the window shows ("Flat UI: the UI scale changes when the fight ends.", "Flat UI: fonts change when the fight ends."), as the cast bar sizes do.

On **Home**, a dim **Every change** at the top right of **Recent changes** lights under the mouse and opens Search on `#changed`, the list of every setting you changed (Recent changes keeps only the last 50). A click on a line of **Recent changes** or **Needs a reload** opens that setting's tab, scrolls to its row and flashes it, as a search result does; the line lights up under the mouse. Each recent change says what it was, from the value before to the value now, as "Off to On" or "300 ms to 450 ms", and pointing at its **Undo** names the value it puts back; a click on **Undo** says in the strip what it did, as "Undid Border colour: back to #000000.". A **Reset page**, and the reload notice's **Undo**, is one line with one Undo for all its settings, and counts as one of the 50 lines kept. Each line of **Needs a reload** has a small **Undo** of its own, dimmed in a fight, that puts that change alone back to its value at login ("Undid Square icons: back to On. It needs no reload now. Ctrl+Z makes it again."), so you can keep **Side tabs** and drop **Square icons**; the line goes, the notice follows, Recent changes shows the Undo as a line of its own, and Ctrl+Z (or that line's Undo) makes the change again. Renaming the profile keeps its recent changes, and a change that a reset, a copy, an import or setup's Finish took away leaves the list, and Ctrl+Z's reach, until its value comes back; Ctrl+Z then says "Nothing to undo. Profiles > Bring back puts Raid back as it was before Reset profile." Search lights the words you typed in each result, says how many results it found under its title, heads each group of results, and each page chip, with its place, as "Flat UI > Colours" beside "Nameplates > Colours" or "Unit frames > Per frame > Player", and counts a module's name as a word of each of its tabs, so "nameplate cast colour" finds the colour rows of **Nameplates > Cast bar**; a chip of a tab shows only when its module's own chip does not already match. Typing `#changed` in Search (or `/flatui #changed`) lists every setting of the profile in use that is off its default, grouped by page, each row working as on its page with its reset arrow; the head says how many ("37 changed settings"), and with none it says "Every setting is at its default." A row you reset stays listed, without its dot, until the next search. Pointing at the Search box says so. A **Profiles** result such as **Import profile** or **In a raid** opens the Profiles page at that row and flashes it. Search also finds the window's own actions, under **Actions**: **Test mode** (**Turn on** or **Turn off**), **Move frames**, **Welcome screen** and **Ready-made looks** (**Open**). The small reset arrow on a changed row names what it puts back, as "Reset to default: On", "Reset to default: 300 ms" or, for a colour, a swatch and its code; it brightens under the mouse, and a click says in the strip what it did, as "Reset Border colour: back to #000000. Ctrl+Z puts it back." ("back to its default" where the value has no words of its own). Each row of the **Profiles** page says what it does when you point at it. **Duplicate**, beside **Create** under **New profile**, copies the profile in use with its frame positions, under the name you type or as "<name> copy", and puts the copy in use, while Create starts from the defaults. Each empty box of the Profiles page says in dim words what goes in it ("Name of the new profile", "New name for Raid", "Press Export, then Ctrl+C", "Press Export chosen, then Ctrl+C" under **Export by module**, "Paste a profile here (Ctrl+V)", "Paste trackers here", and "Name of the new profile" beside **Import as**); a click in an export box, **Export by module**'s too, selects all of its text again, and Ctrl+C there says "Copied. Paste it where you share it." in the strip. Under **Export by module**, **Modules** opens the list of what an export holds (the button reads **Hide list** while it is open), with **All** and **None** above the modules, so None and one tick export a single module; **Export chosen** with nothing ticked says "Tick a module first." The **Fonts** page has a preview band like the module pages: a title, a body line, a number line and small print in the chosen faces, outline, shadow and size beside the game's pop-up text, following each change, and pointing at a line finds its row. The **Flat UI** page has one too, setup's bordered panel with a health and a mana bar in your border, background and **Bar texture**, and its **Colours** tab setup's colour bars (health at 100%, 70%, 40% and 15%, the cast, channel, uninterruptible, interrupted and craft colours, reaction and power), both following each change at once. The positions of a pasted profile are fitted to your screen and UI scale, as those a new profile starts with are, so a frame keeps its share of the screen and none lands off a smaller one. A profile made on a screen of another size shows **Fit positions to this screen** under the paste field, on; turn it off to keep the positions as the text has them. With **Frame positions** off no position is imported, so the Screen line and Fit positions go, and the count of a part left out is dimmed. **Delete profile** asks 'Delete "Raid"?' and says how many other characters use it, who start as new characters at their next login; **Reset profile** says that every setting and every frame position goes back. Each of these questions, and those of **Copy from** and an import with **Replace**, ends with "Bring back puts it back until you reload." (or "them"), as do the tips of those rows, since until you reload none of them is for good: after **Reset profile**, **Copy from**, an import with **Replace** or setup's **Finish**, the **Reset profile** row has **Bring back** beside **Reset**, which puts the profile in use back as it was ("Puts Raid back as it was before Reset profile at 21:04: 124 settings, 9 frame positions."; a second press puts back what the first took), and after **Delete profile** the Delete row has **Bring back**, which puts the deleted profile back with the characters that used it and its **Switch automatically** rules. The strip after each says so: "Reset Raid. Bring back on the Profiles page puts it back until you reload." Picking a profile under **Switch automatically** for the kind of place you are in now asks "You are in a raid now. Switch to Raid now?", with **Switch now** and **Later**; otherwise the switch waits until you next enter such a place. In a fight, **Switch now** waits for its end, as a profile picked by hand does: the strip says "Switches to Raid when the fight ends.", the title bar shows "Raid after the fight", and the profile switches as the fight ends.

Most settings apply at once. When a change needs a reload, wherever you made it, a small notice at the top of the screen says what needs it, with **Reload** (which also works where the game blocks reloads for add-ons, outside a fight), **Undo** and **Later**, which fades it out until your next such change; it fades out by itself when you put the change back. Point at the notice or any of its buttons for every change with its value now and at login, such as "Square icons: Off now, On at login". The **Reload** at the bottom right of the options window reads "Reload (2)" in the accent while changes wait, and pointing at it shows the same list; with nothing waiting it is plain and says nothing needs a reload. **Undo** sets them all back to what the game runs since login, so no reload is needed, and chat says how many it put back (one line of Home's **Recent changes**, which one Ctrl+Z makes again); it is dimmed and waits in a fight and while Setup has the options window. The notice stays clear of the options window's title bar, the `/flatui move` toolbar and the test mode bar, just under them or above them. Move it with the **Reload notice** box in `/flatui move`; while test mode is on and nothing needs a reload, a dimmed sample notice ("Sample: 2 changes need a reload: Square icons, Side tabs.", with greyed buttons) shows at its spot, stepping clear of the same bars. Where the game blocks reloads for add-ons, every **Reload** button of Flat UI is dimmed in a fight and a press says "Flat UI: Reload works here again after the fight. Type /reload to reload now."; it comes back after the fight. There, as everywhere, a Reload's label sinks a pixel while you hold it. The **Ignore** and **Turn off and reload** buttons of the taint log box, and **Open Flat UI** on the game's Settings page, light under the mouse and act only when you let go over them.

After an update, the first login on the account shows **What's new**: a window of Flat UI's own with what changed in each version since the one you last played, newest first (from a version older than 0.5.43, which kept no note of it, 0.5.43 alone), its settings in the accent, with **Open Flat UI's options** and **Close** (Escape closes it too). It waits for the end of a fight, steps aside if one starts and comes back after it, and can be dragged anywhere on screen. **What's new after an update**, under **Updates** on the **Options and setup** tab of the **Flat UI** page, chooses **Show the window** (the default), **One line in chat** ("Flat UI updated to 0.5.43. /flatui new shows what changed.") or **Nothing**. A new install shows nothing, since the welcome screen and setup walk you through Flat UI, and a new character that the welcome screen greets gets the chat line instead. **What's new in 0.5.43** at the bottom of **Home**, and `/flatui new`, open it at any time.

The **Errands** page, on by default, does the chores the game leaves to you: it sells grey items at merchants, and your sell list twelve at a time so the buyback still holds them, repairs with your gold or the guild's where you choose (one chat line says what was sold and repaired, and one how many grey items Shift kept when you held it as the merchant opened), buys a full stack or as many as your gold, the stock and your bags allow after a Shift-click and a question, can answer the merchant's questions before a purchase with tokens or the sale of tradeable loot, loots every slot at once, makes room in full bags with a button that names the grey item it deletes, learns everything at a trainer with one button that says how many it will learn, guards Release Spirit in dungeons, can release your spirit half a second after you die in a battleground (**Release at once in battlegrounds**, off by default; also after a /reload or a relog while dead; not while a soulstone or Reincarnation is offered, never in an arena), types DELETE for you, and can accept summons, resurrections and invites from friends or your guild, decline duels and keep the last mail recipient. The Release Spirit guard, the battleground release, DELETE, summons, resurrections, invites, duels and the mail recipient are on its **Popups and invites** tab. Its **Quests and levelling** tab accepts and hands in quests, marks the quest reward a merchant pays most for with a small gold coin on its icon as the window opens, picks gossip options, offers the new ranks of your spells for your action bars (on a line at the top of the screen that the **New spell ranks line** box of `/flatui move` places), shows the reputation you gain, reminds you of talent points, adds /way (typed with the world map open, the pin goes on as you close it, and /way clear before then drops it and says so) and turns on the game's in-game navigation, its marker over the quest or map pin you track, which Forever's options leave out.

The **Data panels** page, on by default with its two panels off until you show them, adds a top and a bottom panel with bag space, gold across your characters (with what you made or spent this session, and from ten minutes in what that is an hour, in its tooltip, dim after the time it counts from, as "since 21:04"; Ctrl-click on it counts the session from now, for its tooltip and the bag window's gold, and the tooltip's **This session** line stays, at 0c, and grows from there), durability (with how many items are broken in red, a tooltip that lists the lowest first with the repair cost, and no slot where gear does not wear), your ammunition (arrows or bullets, or the stack of a thrown weapon, as "Ammo 1,840", gold at the Trackers page's **Ammunition minimum** and red when out, with the ammunition and the free slots of your quiver in its tooltip; no slot without a bow, crossbow, gun or thrown weapon), a warlock's soul shards (red at none, with the soul bag's free slots in its tooltip), your hearthstone, coordinates, the time (local or realm, 12 or 24 hours, as the game's clock is set), the quests in your log (red when it is full, with those ready to hand in and their experience in the tooltip), your experience (the share of the level and the rested share in blue, with what is left, the quests ready to hand in and the time to level in its tooltip; nothing at the top level), your reputation (the faction Flat UI's reputation bar shows, else the one the game watches, with its standing in colour and the share done, and in its tooltip what is left to the next standing and the reputation pace), frame rate and latency, friends and guild online, and the equipment set you wear, which a click changes. A click on bag space opens or closes Flat UI's bags; a click on the time opens the stopwatch (Shift-click the calendar), and one on the quest log, friends, guild, durability, reputation or coordinates opens the quest log, the friends list, the guild window, the character window, your reputations or the world map, in a fight as it ends, with a line in chat that says so; Shift-click on coordinates, experience or reputation puts the line in chat; and a right click on any of these slots (Alt-right-click on another addon's feed, whose plain clicks stay its own) opens a small menu under it, titled with the panel and the slot ("Top panel, third slot"), to choose what the slot shows, the one shown now in gold with a tick; what shows nothing for this character reads dim with why, as "Soul shards (only for a warlock)", and a pick that leaves the slot with no room says so in chat. **Move left** and **Move right** in it swap the slot with its nearest shown neighbour and keep the menu open on it, so each press moves it on. While the panel has a slot set to None, **Add a slot after this one** opens a second menu ("Top panel, new third slot") whose pick lands right beside the slot you clicked, and the panel grows at once. Each move or add is one line in Recent changes, and Ctrl+Z takes it back. A long menu, with many feeds or a large Text size, goes on in a second column beside the first, as soon as it would run past the room below a top panel or above a bottom one, so it never covers its own panel. Its last line, **Data panels options**, opens the page. The slot under the mouse lights up faintly, and each change of your gold floats from the Gold slot as "+38s 2c" in green or "-1g 4s" in red (a quick run of changes as one sum; below the slot on a panel at the top of the screen). Each panel can be wider than its slots and sit over the game's windows, its text can take an outline, a shadow and your class colour or your own, and it can fade in or out of combat, easing back to full under the mouse in a tenth of a second and settling again as it leaves, and coming back to full while `/flatui move` or Edit Mode is open. Frame rate and latency on the panels and by the minimap turn yellow and red when poor, and Flat UI's reputation bar can show reputation per hour and the time to the next standing.

The **Threat list** page, on by default, lists your group by threat on the enemy you fight, highest first, in class colours; at 100% a member has aggro. Your own bar has a thin white outline, and with **Always show your bar** it takes the place of the last bar when you rank lower, your place before your name ("12. Jon"). As damage or healer, while you are not tanking and pass **Warn at** (90%), your bar pulses red and a sound plays once for that enemy, and once you hold its aggro your bar keeps a steady red wash (the sound plays then if it has not, so a sudden pull is heard too); as a tank (your group role, else your stance or form) **Warn when you lose aggro** pulses it red for up to 2 seconds, until you take it back (the red stops as your taunt lands), and plays the sound once when an enemy you held turns to someone else, while the bar of whoever took it pulses red with yours and the title names them in red ("Thistlefur Avenger > Ivo") until the red stops, so the one to taunt off is marked at a glance (neither on bosses, where the game hides threat; in test mode the sample Mage's bar pulses with yours at each of their turns, the title reading "Sample > Mage"). As a tank, **Show who is near your aggro** (on by default) turns the bar of any member at **Warn at** or above who is not tanking a steady amber, with no sound, so you see the Mage at 96% before their next crit and can taunt or ask them to slow down; in test mode the sample Rogue climbs to 94% and turns amber between the Mage's turns. With two tanks, **Calm when another tank takes it** (on by default) keeps all of that quiet for the other tank: when the warrior or the bear who takes an enemy from you is a tank (their tank role, the raid's main tank, or in a party their tank stance, form or aura), no sound plays, neither bar pulses, and their bar never turns amber as they build threat; anyone else taking it still sets everything off. In test mode the sample Tank takes the enemy from you for one step after the Rogue's, with no pulse and no sound while this is on, and with it off both bars pulse red there, so the row can be judged. The title shows the enemy's raid marker, such as a Skull, before its name, so you can tell which of three Defias Pillagers the list is about. With **Follow your tank's target** (on by default), while neither your target nor your target's target is an enemy, the list shows the enemy your group's tank fights (their tank role, the raid's main tank, or in a party their tank stance, form or aura), so a healer who heals by clicking party frames, with no target or another healer targeted, still sees it in a fight, and it turns to the tank's next enemy within a second. With **Pets** off the samples of test mode and `/flatui threat` leave out the sample Wolf and Voidwalker too. With **List** at **Your target, then enemies you are losing** (the default), the list always shows your group on your target as above, with all its rows, colours, warnings and sounds, and while you are a tank (read as above, or by **Your role** on the **Threat** tab of **Nameplates**) it adds what happens around it: under a thin line, up to 4 small rows for the other enemies with a nameplate that you are losing, the **Lost** ones first, then the **Slipping** ones, each with its raid marker, its name, its colour and pulse, and who holds it or closes in ("Mage 112%"), and the title reads "Defias Pillager · 1 lost" while one of them is lost. While no other enemy is lost or slipping nothing is added, so a fight on one enemy looks as it always did; the small rows count apart from **Number of bars**, and one that turns to Lost flashes and plays **Sound when you lose an enemy**, while your target itself keeps **Warn when you lose aggro**. **Enemies you fight** shows the enemies instead of the group, in every role: a row for each enemy with a nameplate that fights you or your group, up to **Number of bars**, and a last line that counts the rest ("+3 more, 1 lost"). Each row has the enemy's raid marker, its name and a bar of your hold on it, your threat against the highest of the others', full while no one else has threat on it, half-way at an even split and empty once another has twice yours, with a faint tick at the half and at **Slipping at**. An enemy someone else holds is **Lost**: red, its row pulses fast and it names who holds it in their class colour with their share of your threat ("Mage 112%"); one where another member passes **Slipping at** (80% of your threat, under the **Enemies** heading) is **Slipping**: amber, a slower pulse and who closes in ("Rogue 86%"); the rest are **Held**: green, calm and a little dimmer, with the closest share. The colours are those of **Threat you do not want**, **Threat changing hands** and **Threat you want** of nameplate threat, so the list and the plates agree. Lost enemies come first, then Slipping ones, closest first, then Held ones, so the top row is the one to take back; your target's row has a thin white outline, the title reads "Threat: 3 enemies, 1 lost" (red while one is lost), and an enemy you held that turns to Lost flashes and plays **Sound when you lose an enemy** (by default **Warning sound**, the sound of **Warning sound** while **Warn when you lose aggro** is on, as in the group list). With **Calm when another tank takes it** an enemy another tank holds stays calm in **Another tank's colour**. The rows take no clicks, so nothing in a fight waits on them; pointing at one says who has it and to click the enemy's nameplate to target it. The enemies are read at most five times a second, only in a fight (under your target only while you tank) and nothing out of one, and on bosses, where the game hides threat, a row shows what the game gives. **Your group on your target** shows your group alone, never the enemies under it. In test mode and with `/flatui threat`, **Your target, then enemies you are losing** shows the sample group with two sample Defias under it, one lost and one slipping, in every role so you can see them, and in test mode every 6 seconds the slipping one is lost and flashes, the lost one is taken back and leaves, and the third starts to slip; **Enemies you fight** shows four sample Defias, one lost, one slipping and two held, and in test mode every 6 seconds the slipping one is lost, the lost one is taken back and a held one starts to slip, so the flash, the sound (once each time test mode comes on) and the rows moving can be seen. Both lists, and the small rows under your target, move with **Animate bars** (on by default): a bar slides to its new value over a fifth of a second, a row glides to its new place as the order changes and flashes as it overtakes another, the top bar carries a faint sheen, and your group's names take a light shade of their class colour; off, the bars jump and stand still, and the warnings still pulse. The **Healer mana** page, on by default, shows your healers' mana in dungeons and raids. In a group where nobody picked the healer role it counts the healing classes, but leaves out yourself while your talents are in a damage or tank tree (a Retribution paladin, a Feral druid), and anyone in their tank stance, form or aura (Righteous Fury, Bear Form), read again as talents change and after each fight, so the list, its average and ready are about the real healer. **Trinket queue**, a page of its own under **Character and gear**, on by default, swaps your trinkets for you. Each trinket slot has its own list, best first. A trinket with 30 seconds or less of its cooldown left counts as ready (**Ready with this much left**), since putting on a trinket with a use starts a 30 second cooldown on it anyway, and one just put on is never swapped for a lower one in that time. A trinket with no use can be in the list too, and is worn while nothing above it is ready. **Delay** keeps a trinket on that long after you use it, so its buff runs out first; **Stop** keeps it on while you wear it. The game changes no trinket in a fight, so a swap waits for its end, and also while you cast, are dead, hold an item on the cursor or your bags are busy. Pointing at a spell in the spellbook or at a talent pulses each of Flat UI's buttons that holds it, or its flyout, with the game's highlight (flat gold over the icon with **Flat highlights**), in a fight too, as the game does on its own buttons, so you can find where a spell sits or see that it is on no bar. **Aim highlight** (on by default, on the **Glows and animations** page) makes the button of a spell you place on the ground, such as Blizzard, Rain of Fire, Flamestrike, Hurricane or Volley, pulse softly in the cast bar's **Cast colour** (or your class colour while **Class colour on the player bar** paints the player cast bar, which the button's cast fill then takes too, so bar and button agree) while the cursor waits for its spot, until you place, cast or cancel it; an Auto Shot or a wand shot meanwhile leaves it pulsing. It no longer depends on the game's reticle event: Flat UI also asks the game whether a spell is waiting for its spot or for a click on its target, so a heal or a curse cast with no target to take it pulses too, and a stance, form or aura already lit stays out of it. **Interrupt flash** flashes in the cast bar's **Interrupted colour** (on the **Colours** page), so the button and the cast bar say "interrupted" in one colour. The cast fill, the interrupt flash, the aim highlight, **Flash when ready** and the game glow of **Proc glow** light only buttons you can see, never a button past a bar's **Number of icons** in Edit Mode. With the options window open and test mode off, turning **Cast fill animation**, **Interrupt flash** or **Aim highlight** on plays it once on the first sample button (the first button of Flat UI's main bar that holds something, else of bars 2 to 8), and a change of **Proc glow** (but **None**) or **Proc border colour** lights that button's proc glow for 3 seconds in the look chosen, the soft or game glow of **Glow style** or the border in **Proc border colour**; a colour dragged keeps it up.

On the **Button text** page, **Count colour** (white by default) and **Count position** (**Bottom right**, as the game has it, **Bottom left**, **Top left**, **Top right** or **Bottom**) colour and move the count of reagents, arrows or stacks on Flat UI's buttons and the restyled game buttons, so it stays clear of a hotkey or a macro name in the bottom right; a new position made in a fight waits for its end. **Macro name colour** (white by default) and **Macro name position** (**Bottom**, as the game has it, **Top** or **Centre**) do the same for the macro name, so a long name can sit at the top, clear of a count at the bottom; the colour applies at once, in a fight too, and a new position made in a fight waits for its end. The first such change in a fight, made with the options window open, says so in chat: "Flat UI: hotkeys, macro names and counts move when the fight ends."

The **Hidden frames** page (on, with nothing ticked) takes frames of the game off the screen: tick one and it is gone, untick it and it is back as it was. It lists the game's HUD frames that `/flatui move` moves, in groups (action bars, unit frames, minimap, buffs and debuffs, bars and timers, messages and alerts, trackers and meters), and the parts of the minimap one by one: the zone name, the tracking, mail, calendar, clock, addon and zoom buttons. The chat, the loot window, the tooltip, the windows (Character, Spellbook, bags and the rest) and Flat UI's own frames are not on it. The pet frame, the totems and the class resource bars go with the player frame while Edit Mode keeps them under it, and their boxes in `/flatui move` say so. **Alerts** takes each toast away as it comes. A hidden frame stays hidden when the game shows it again, also after a reload, and each tick's tip says what goes with it and what still works, as the keys of a hidden action bar, or B and the bag keys without the bag bar. In `/flatui move`, Shift and right-click a box to hide its frame (on a box with no tick it only says so in chat), or press **Hide frame** in the inspector; the **Hidden** group at the top of the frame list shows a frame again with its eye. A change in a fight waits for its end. While **Leave the game's action bars alone** is on, the game's bars stay; the micro menu and the bag bar hide either way, and the keys such as C, P and L still open their windows. The main bar and the frames the game stacks above it, such as the cast bar, keep their places with the micro menu hidden. `/flatui move` still does not move the micro menu while that row is on, since the game hangs its main bar from it. **Fading** leaves a hidden frame alone: it comes back at its own opacity and fades again from there. `/flatui hide` opens the page.

The game's windows open clear of your unit frames: where the game would put a window's title over the player or target frame at the top of the screen, as it does with the Character window, the Spellbook, the map and Professions, the window opens just under them, with any window beside it at the same height (**Open windows under the unit frames**, on by default in the **Windows** section of **Movers**). A window you drag by its title stays where you put it, and a unit frame you moved lower down leaves the windows where the game puts them. Nothing of the game's window layout is changed: in a fight a window the game protects stays where the game opens it.

On the **Windows** page the Spellbook has dark flat pages with light text, as the other restyled windows: spell names in white, the lines under them in grey, headers and the page number in gold. **Spellbook parchment**, off by default in **Window art**, gives the game's parchment pages and their dark text back. The quest giver, gossip, book and letter pages and the quest log details are dark with light text too, by default: **Dark quest, gossip and book pages**, on in **Window art**, sets the game's own Quest Text Contrast to Dark for your account, and the game draws its dark page and light text itself. Off, the parchment and the contrast you had before come back; a page already open changes the next time it opens, and `/flatui status` says when the game kept its own value.

Loot rolls get Flat UI's look with **Loot rolls**, on by default on the **Quests and loot** page: a flat background and thin border, the item's icon cropped with a border in its quality colour, the name in that colour, a flat timer bar with a thin border, and **Need**, **Greed** and **Pass** in a row; off gives the game's look back. With **Seconds left on rolls**, on by default, the whole seconds left to roll count down at the right end just above the timer ("39", "38" ...), and under 10 seconds the number and the bar turn red; the number goes away as the roll ends or the Need animation plays, and the setup picture and test mode's sample rolls show it too. **Sound before a roll ends** (None by default, or Spell alert, Chimes, Bell, Warhorn, Raid warning, Alarm or Whisper warning) plays once as a roll you have not rolled on comes down to 5 seconds left, in a fight too, so a roll that came in during a pull is not passed unseen; several rolls ending together give one sound, picking a sound in the options plays it, and test mode's sample rolls play it once. Move them with the **Loot rolls** box in `/flatui move`: until you do, the game places them, and once you have, they stack from there, up or down as **Rolls stack** says. Right-click the box to give them back to the game.

**Hide error messages**, on by default on the **Messages and timers** page, hides the kinds of red error messages you choose in the middle of the screen, with their sound and voice: not enough mana, rage or energy, out of range, facing the wrong way, not ready yet, no or an invalid target, not while moving, and, off by default, not in line of sight, **While stunned, silenced or feared** (also pacified, confused or charmed: "Can't do that while stunned", "Can't attack while fleeing" and the like, from keys you press while you cannot act) and **Wrong stance or form** ("Must be in Battle Stance", "Can't use items while shapeshifted" and the like, from a key of a stance or form you are not in; an NPC that will not talk to you while shapeshifted still says so). **When**, under it, is **Always** by default; **Only in a fight** hides them only in combat and shows every message out of it, so you still learn why a cast on a mob or a mailbox failed. **Error text size** (100%, 50% to 150%), under it, sizes those red lines and the yellow quest lines at once, also with Hide error messages off; with the options window open a change prints a sample "Not enough mana" in red there at the new size, at most once a second while you drag. **Hide the zone title**, **Hide the subzone title** and **Hide the talking head**, off by default on the same page, take away the big zone name and the talking head with its voice. **Zone title size** (100%, 50% to 150%) on the same page makes the zone name, its PvP line and the subzone name smaller or larger, for a player who wants to see where they are without the huge letters; 100% gives the game's size back. With the options window open and test mode off, a change shows "Elwynn Forest", "(Contested Territory)" and "Goldshire" at their real place and new size for 3 seconds, then they fade out. In test mode, and with **Samples** on in `/flatui move`, the **Zone text** box shows those three lines in the game's colours at Zone title size, leaving out a part whose Hide row is on, and the **Error text** box a red "Not enough mana" over a yellow "Kobold Vermin slain: 3/8" at Error text size, so neither box is placed blind. **Clear the damage meter**, off by default on the same page, clears the game's damage meter as you enter a dungeon or raid, or asks first. **Seconds on the timers**, on by default on the same page with **Breath and fatigue timers**, puts the time left ("1:12", "0:42") at the right end of the label box under the breath, fatigue and feign death bars, with the label still centred; while the bar drains the time turns red under 10 seconds, and it stays white while your breath comes back.

**Hit table under the player frame**, on the **Hit table** page, under **Character and gear**, shows under your player frame your chances against your target to miss, be dodged, parried or blocked, glance, crit and hit, worked out again as your gear, buffs, weapon skill, form and target change: a line for your swings (each hand while you dual wield), your special attacks, your ranged attacks and your spells, picked by your class and form, with the crit cap of your swings, yellow once your crit is past it; a target whose level shows as ?? counts as three levels above you. Your spells also get **Resist**, no part of the roll: the share of a spell's damage resisted on average, with your Spell Piercing. The enemy's own resistances are not known and count as none, so with piercing it goes below 0, in green, for the damage a creature takes more (WoW: Forever's Spell Vulnerability, half of what a point of resistance takes, a point below 0; against a player piercing does nothing below 0); and a creature above you resists about 2% more a level, Classic's rule, an estimate on WoW: Forever that piercing does not lower, which a spell that also slows or roots pays with its higher miss instead. The game gives only the piercing of every school, so piercing of one school, as Fire Piercing, counts in the gear comparison and a spell's Expected line and not here; `/flatui status` says what Resist counts. By default it is **For tanks and damage dealers**: it shows while you count as a tank or a damage dealer and hides while you count as a healer, reading your role as Flat UI setup does (your group role, else a tank stance, form or aura, else the talent tree you put most points in, else your class: a priest heals), and follows a new group role, your talents, a switch of dual spec, your level and a change of form or aura; `/flatui status` says which role you count as and why. **Always** and **Off** hold whatever your role. Where **Show** hides it without an enemy targeted or out of a fight, it fades in over 0.15 s and out over 0.35 s, and an enemy targeted again during the fade brings it back at once. The table hangs under the player frame, and under the pet frame, the totems and a cast bar Edit Mode locks there; `/flatui move` gives it a spot of its own, as every new profile has from Jon Default, and wherever it is it steps down under your pet frame, or test mode's sample of it, where it would run into it, and back up when the pet goes. **On enemy tooltips**, on the same page and also **For tanks and damage dealers** by default, adds your hit table to the tooltip of an enemy you can attack: a line for each attack, as in "Swing  M8 D6.5 G40 C12.1 H33.4" (M miss, D dodge, P parry, G glance, B block, C crit, H hit, in percent; R on the Spells line, its Resist, as in R-2.5, with a dim note of what it counts while it is not 0), against that enemy's level (a level shown as ??, or a boss of your level or above, counts as three levels above you), with parries and blocks only from the front (a tank counts in front), following **Attacks**, **Special attacks**, **Where you stand** and **Hit cap against a boss** whatever **Hit table under the player frame** is set to; those rows stay open while it is **Off**. In a fight the game hides your stats, so the numbers there are from before it, and a dim line says so. With **Only while Ctrl is held**, under it and on by default, the lines show only with Ctrl: held as the tooltip shows, or pressing Ctrl adds the lines at once; they leave when the tooltip next changes, not as you let go (over a unit frame, whose tooltip the game sets again every 0.2 seconds, they go with the key). The tooltip is never built again for them, so it is otherwise as the game has it; **Hint while Ctrl is up** (on by default) ends an enemy's tooltip with one dim line, "Ctrl: your hit table against it", in their place meanwhile, as items and spells have their Shift hints, and it becomes their header as you press Ctrl. It needs **Chat and tooltips** on. **Text size**, a dropdown on the same page, sets the size of the table's text, from 70% to 200%; a pick applies at once and is kept with your profile. **Text size on enemy tooltips** does the same for the hit table's lines on enemy tooltips alone: the tooltip's other lines, and every tooltip after it, keep the game's size.

The **World and cursor** page, under **Helpers**, can put a pulsing glow on the herb, vein or quest object your Interact Key would use, in gold or coloured by what it is (herbs green, ore copper, quest objects gold, loot white), at **Glow size** (100%, 50% to 200%, the glow, its ring and its stars together, changing at once on an object that glows), fading in as it lands and, with **Glow sound**, ringing once out of a fight, with a row that turns on the game's Interact Key and its icons the glow needs in one pick, let the camera zoom out farther than the game's own options allow (ticking it glides the camera out to the new distance), and draw a ring around the mouse pointer, always or in combat only (fading in and out with the fight), with your cast running round it and, if you like, the global cooldown; the ring hides while you turn the camera with the mouse, and with **Grow when you shake the mouse** a quick shake swells it for a moment so you find the pointer. **When you shake the mouse**, the third choice of **Cursor ring**, shows no ring until you shake the pointer: then it fades in swollen, eases back to its size and fades out about a second later. With **In combat only** or **When you shake the mouse**, changing **Cursor ring**, its size or its colour out of a fight shows the ring round the pointer for about a second, then fades it out. Changing **Cast progress colour** or **Global cooldown colour**, or ticking **Cast progress on the ring** or **Global cooldown on the ring**, out of a fight plays a sample on the ring for about 2.6 seconds, a 2.5 second cast running round it and a 1.5 second global cooldown inside, in the new colours, also with **In combat only**; a drag of the colour keeps it going. Picking a **Glow sound** or a **Jump sound** for the lava tick timer plays it. The unit frames can hide the hit numbers the game flashes over your portrait and your pet's.

**Lava tick timer**, on the same page and **In lava zones** by default, helps you walk or swim through lava. Lava burns you every 2 seconds, and a jump timed so you are in the air as the tick lands takes no damage, even when you land back in it. After a fire hit on you in Molten Core, Blackrock Depths or Spire, Ragefire Chasm, Searing Gorge, Burning Steppes or Un'Goro Crater (or anywhere, with **Anywhere**), a bar under your character counts down to the next tick and turns green with **Jump** for the last **Jump lead** (400 ms by default), with a flash and, if you pick one, a **Jump sound**. Each tick says **Hit**, and a tick you were in the air for says **Dodged**, then **Dodged x2**, **x3** as the dodges in a row build (a hit starts again; `/flatui lava` names your best this session); the bar goes 5 seconds after the last one. It is an estimate: the game tells add-ons that fire hit you, not that it was lava, so in a fight it counts only fire hits that keep the 2-second rhythm, and the first tick of each visit always lands. `/flatui move` places it, `/flatui lava` says what it sees, `/flatui lava test` shows a sample and `/flatui lava trace` prints each hit on you. Changing one of its rows out of a fight shows the sample bar in the new look for about 4 seconds, silent and over the options window, then fades it out; in test mode it then drops back under the game's windows and keeps sampling there. The idea is the timing bar of the Classic WeakAura "Avoid lava damage"; none of its code is used.

## Install

1. Download the newest file from [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/projects/1750813) or from the releases of this repository.
2. Put the `FlatUI` folder in the `Interface\AddOns` folder of your WoW: Forever install.
3. Start the game and type `/flatui`.

## Commands

- `/flatui` opens the settings window.
- `/flatui setup` walks round your screen in six short stops and saves what you pick.
- `/flatui profiles` shows the ready-made looks. A click puts one in use, and **Back to** on the card that follows puts the profile you had back in use.
- `/flatui new` (or `/flatui changes`) shows what changed in this version of Flat UI; `/flatui new 0.5.41` shows that version, one of the newest six.
- `/flatui move` shows a box for every frame. Drag a box to move its frame; Shift and right-click the box of a HUD frame of the game to hide it. A name too long for its box is cut short with "...", and a box too small for one has none; point at a box to see it drawn over the others with its whole name. Given in a fight (also from the options window's **Move frames** row or test mode's **Move** button), it says "Flat UI: /flatui move opens when the fight ends." and the boxes open as the fight ends; a second one in the same fight takes that back. Boxes a fight paused ("Flat UI: Movers pause for combat.") open again as it ends; `/flatui move` in that fight says "Flat UI: /flatui move stays closed after the fight." and they stay closed. `/flatui grab` picks the frame under the mouse to move; in a fight it says "Flat UI: /flatui grab starts when the fight ends.", the boxes open with the pick running as it ends, and a second one takes it back. Every chat line of the movers starts with "Flat UI:". Point at a control of its toolbar (the grid's **Off**, **Dim** and **Bright**, **Snap**, **Samples**, **Frame list**, **Undo**, **Redo**, **Done**) for what it does; **Undo** says how many steps it holds ("Puts back the last change, 3 to undo."), and Ctrl-Z does the same while the boxes are open. **Redo**, between **Undo** and **Done**, or Ctrl-Y or Ctrl-Shift-Z, puts back what Undo just took away, step by step, a **Space across** or **Space down** as one step, and so is a **Reset all** or a **Centre ticked**, however many frames it changed, until you make a new move; its tooltip says how many steps can be redone ("Puts back what Undo took away, 2 to redo. Ctrl-Y does the same."), and **Undo** and **Redo** are dim while they hold nothing. A press on the toolbar, a row of the frame list or its eye, the buttons of **Keep your changes?** and of the lower movers bar, and a right-click on a box counts only when you let go over what you pressed, as in the options window, and a toolbar button's label sinks 1 px while held. The lower movers panel has the toolbar's look: its card background and the options window's text colours, and **Pick a frame**, **Reset all**, **Centre ticked** and **Done** (in the accent) light under the mouse, darken and sink while held, take a new accent the next time the boxes open and say what they do when you point at them ("Puts every moved frame back where the game has it. Click twice; Undo takes it back."). The grid's segments, **Snap** and **Samples** light up under the mouse, and Snap and Samples click and slide as the options window's switches do.
- `/flatui hide` opens the Hidden frames page, where a tick hides a frame of the game.
- `/flatui status` says what is styled, and any error.
- `/flatui sell <item>` always sells an item at merchants (an item link or ID). Type it again to stop. `/flatui sell` alone lists the items. **Items always sold** on the Errands page takes one off.
- `/flatui keep <item>` never sells a grey item, and Make room never deletes it. **Items never sold** takes one off.
- `/flatui words add <word>` colours a word in other players' chat lines as your name is; `remove <word>` stops it, and `/flatui words` lists them.
- `/flatui copy` opens a box with the last 50 chat lines heard, ready to copy; `/flatui copy 200` takes up to 200.
- `/flatui train` learns everything you can pay for at an open trainer, like the Train all button.
- `/flatui npc` skips the NPC you talk to or target: its quests and gossip are left to you. Type it again to stop.
- `/flatui gossip` lists the options of the NPC you talk to. `/flatui gossip 2` picks the second option for you from now on.
- `/flatui uprank` offers the new ranks of your spells for your action bars.
- `/flatui rank <spell>` keeps that rank where it is on your bars (a spell link or ID).
- `/way 45 61` puts a pin on your map and the game points you to it; `/way Elwynn Forest 45 61` for another zone, `/way clear` to take it off. Typed while the world map is open, it waits and goes on (or comes off) as you close the map. `/flatui way` does the same when another addon has `/way`.
- `/flatui xp` says your experience per hour, time to level and kills and quests to level, and your reputation per hour and time to the next standing with the faction on Flat UI's bar. `/flatui xp reset`, or a Ctrl-click on the bar, starts counting again.
- `/flatui threat` shows sample bars of the threat list for a few seconds, out of combat, or the four sample enemies while the list shows Enemies; `/flatui healers` does the same for the healer mana list, also out of combat only.
- `/flatui lava` says what the lava tick timer sees: the place and its IDs, the ticks seen and dodged, the last hit on you and the gaps between ticks. `/flatui lava test` shows a sample for 8 seconds, and `/flatui lava trace` prints a line for each hit on you, to check in lava that the game reports its ticks.
- `/flatui cooldown add healthstone` puts your healthstones on the cooldown row, every rank as one icon; `healing` and `mana` do the same for potions, and `/flatui cooldown remove` takes one off.
- `/flatui cooldown sound <spell> bell` gives one icon of the cooldown row its own ready sound; `row` gives it back the row's.
- `/flatui cooldown add item <item>` puts an item with a use, such as the Hearthstone, on the cooldown row (an ID, a link or a name); `remove item` takes it off. `/flatui cooldown import 1` adds the spells with a cooldown on action bar 1 (2 to 8 likewise).
- `/flatui pulse test` shows a cooldown pulse; `/flatui pulse ignore <spell or item>` leaves one out and `/flatui pulse watch` puts it back; `/flatui pulse list` says what pulses.
- `/flatui pulse sound <spell or item> bell` gives one its own pulse sound; `none` silences it, and `page` gives it the page's sound again.
- `/flatui pulse spot <spell or item> 2` sends one to pulse spot 2; `1` puts it back.
- `/flatui role` says whether nameplate threat reads you as a tank and why; `tank`, `damage`, `auto` or `toggle` sets it.
- `/flatui gear` opens or closes the Gear comparison window; `/flatui gear <item>` compares a shift-clicked item there, as an Alt-click on an item link in chat does. The window's **Find** button opens the item finder. `/flatui gear add <spell>` (an ID, a name or a shift-clicked spell, which keeps its rank), `remove <spell>`, `bar 1` and `reset` change the spells it compares, `weight <spell> <0 to 5>` sets how much a spell counts in the **Overall** line, and `list` prints them.
- `/flatui queue` opens or closes the Trinket queue window (`/flatui trinkets` too); `/flatui queue top` or `bottom` opens it on that slot's list, `/flatui queue pause` pauses or resumes every swap, `/flatui queue now` lets the queue act at once on a slot you changed by hand, `/flatui queue list` prints both lists, `/flatui queue copy bottom` fills the Bottom list from the Top one (or `copy top` the other way), and `/flatui queue clear top` or `bottom` empties a list.
- `/flatui tracker add group` tracks your class buff on your group; `/flatui tracker back` puts the tracker you last removed back; `/flatui tracker use 2 <item>` sets what weapon tracker 2 applies; `/flatui tracker set 1 health 40` shows pet tracker 1's health in red under 40%; `/flatui tracker set 3 kit bell` gives tracker 3 a sound of its own, which the **Sound** row of **Edit a tracker** also sets.
- `/flatui bags` opens or closes the Flat UI bag window; `/flatui bags <name>` shows another of your characters' bags.
- `/flatui bank` opens or closes your bank window: live at the banker with Flat UI's bank window, else as you last left it; `/flatui bank <name>` shows another character's bank.
- `/flatui test` turns test mode on or off (`/flatui test on` and `/flatui test off` also work); see **Test mode** below.
- `/flatui test icons` shows three sample icons for 8 seconds on Defensives on you, Your buffs, Party cooldowns and Enemy cooldowns; on the last two they flash and sound at their end as each page's **Flash when ready** and **Sound** say.
- `/flatui scale 80` sets the UI scale it names: from 65 to 115 it turns **Pixel-perfect scale** and **Scale below 65%** off and uses **Custom scale** (with Scale below 65% on since login, the reload notice shows, as that row needs a reload once it is off), and from 35 to 64 it uses **Small scale** with **Scale below 65%** on. Chat says what came of it in the words of `/flatui status`, as "Flat UI: Custom UI scale: 80%. /reload puts the game's scale back."; in a fight it waits for the fight's end and says so. `/flatui scale off` gives the game its own scale back.
- `/flatui profile` names the profile in use and the others ("Flat UI: profile Default. Others: Raid, Alt."); `/flatui profile Raid` puts Raid in use ("Flat UI: using Raid."), in a fight as the fight ends, and a name it does not know lists the profiles ("Flat UI: no profile named Raidd. Profiles: Default, Raid, Alt.").
- Any other words, as `/flatui border colour`, open the options window with them in **Search**: the results show lit, Enter opens the first, and a miss shows the closest pages. `/flatui #changed` lists every setting you changed. While setup has the window, chat says to search once it is closed.
- `/flatui help` (or `/flatui ?`) lists the commands.

## Test mode

Test mode shows your frames on screen with sample content while you change options, so a setting can be seen as you change it, the frames that are hidden until a fight, a target or a group included. Turn it on with the **Test mode** switch on the title bar of the options window, left of Close; **Samples** on the toolbar of `/flatui move`; `/flatui test`; or a key (Key Bindings > Flat UI > **Flat UI test mode**). It does not turn on while Setup has the options window. Asked for in a fight, it says "Flat UI: test mode comes on when the fight ends." (the switch shows its knob on, dimmed, while it waits) and turns on by itself as the fight ends, if what asked for it is still open (the options window, `/flatui move`); asking a second time in the same fight takes that back ("Flat UI: test mode stays off.").

A bar at the top of the screen (under the `/flatui move` toolbar while that shows, and just above or under the options window where it would cover that window's title bar; drag it to put it elsewhere, and it opens there again after a reload) has **Group** (**Solo**, **Party**, **Raid 10**, **Raid 20** or **Raid 40**; Party at first, then the one last picked on the account, also after a reload), **Moving** (on; off holds every sample still so a look can be judged), **Bags**, **Bank**, **Gear**, **Finder** and **Queue**, which open Flat UI's own windows with their real content, **Move**, which opens `/flatui move` with the samples kept on so each frame can be placed where it shows, and **End**. A double-click on the bar's title puts a dragged bar back at its own place and forgets the drag. The bar fades in as test mode comes on and out as it goes off, and turning test mode on again while it fades out brings it straight back. Each control lights up under the mouse and has a one-line tooltip that says what it does. A dim line under it names any frame that has no place yet ("open Edit Mode once"): a frame the game has never placed gets no sample at a corner.

A frame ticked on the **Hidden frames** page gets no sample, and neither does a frame that hangs from it (the pet frame, the totems and a cast bar locked to the player frame).

It ends when you press the switch, **Samples**, `/flatui test`, the key or **End** again; when you close the options window it was turned on from (not while `/flatui move` holds that window); when `/flatui move` closes for good after turning it on (unless the options window is open, which then keeps it); when Setup takes the options window; and at a reload. A fight pauses it, and it comes back after the fight while what turned it on is still open. It saves nothing: no setting, game setting, key binding, target or focus, and no number counts in a statistic; only the bar's own Group and place are kept, for the account.

What does not show, and why:

- **The game's own unit frames are never shown or given a unit**, and **Edit Mode is never opened by Flat UI**: the game protects them, and doing either from an addon taints them and can block targeting. The samples are Flat UI's own frames drawn where the game's are, so a sample can differ slightly from the real frame.
- **Real nameplates are not forced on screen** (that would change game settings), so threat colours show on the sample plate, not on your target's plate.
- **The game's windows** (Character, Spellbook, talents and the rest) are not opened: an addon opening them can taint the game's window manager. Open them yourself and the **Windows** page restyles them as you change it.
- **No drawing exists** for mirror timers, the loot window, the queue panel, alerts, zone text, the talking head, the extra action button, arena and vehicle frames, and Errands' overlays need the NPC's window open. Chat and tooltips get no sample: a printed line would skip the chat filters, and a tooltip shows on any mouse-over anyway.
- **Party cooldown rows hang in the raid stack**, not beside the party samples.

## Test

`FlatUI_test.lua` runs the addon in a simulated client. It needs Lua 5.4.

```
lua5.4 FlatUI_test.lua FlatUI
```

The last line must say `N passed, 0 failed`.

## Code map

`tools/dev/flatui_map.py` builds the code map of Flat UI as one page: each file with its functions, its settings, its calls to other files, and the game's frames, functions, events and CVars it uses. It needs Python 3, Lua 5.4 and git.

```
python3 tools/dev/flatui_map.py --fetch
```

`--fetch` clones Forever's interface source and the client lists of [wowless](https://github.com/wowless/wowless), which name the game's functions, into `tools/dev/sources`, or brings them up to date. The page is `tools/dev/out/flat-ui-map.html`, with its data beside it.

## Spell data

`FlatUI/SpellData.lua` holds the spell values of the spell lines and the gear comparison. `tools/dev/make_spelltips.py` makes it from the inputs in `tools/dev/spelltips`. After a client build or a hotfix, read the client's spell rows again from a new capture of wowsims/forever (`assets/db_inputs/spell_store_inputs.json`, with GrunzForever's export for the few rows it lacks), then make the file:

```
python3 tools/dev/spelltips_import.py spell_store_inputs.json.gz wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite
python3 tools/dev/make_spelltips.py
```

It needs Python 3. `make_spelltips.py --check` writes nothing; it lists every value of the inputs the new rows contradict.

## Rotation data

`FlatUI/RotationData.lua` holds the WoWSims rotations the gear comparison's **Overall** line counts: each ability's uses a minute and its share of the damage and threat, for each class and spec, and the share of the main hand's swings Heroic Strike and Maul take. `tools/dev/make_rotations.py` makes it from `tools/dev/rotations/data.json`, the results of the WoWSims Classic simulator's command line run with each spec's presets, and `FlatUI/SpellData.lua`, whose keys it matches the abilities to:

```
python3 tools/dev/make_rotations.py
```

It needs Python 3. `--check` writes nothing; it says whether the file is what the data makes.

## Item index

`FlatUI/ItemIndex.lua` holds the armor and weapons the item finder searches: each item's ID, English name, class and subclass, required level, item level, quality and the classes that may wear it, one string a slot, read only when the finder first searches that slot. `tools/dev/make_itemindex.py` makes it from GrunzForever's export of the client tables (`wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite`), leaving out the client's test and placeholder items:

```
python3 tools/dev/make_itemindex.py wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite
```

It needs Python 3. `--check` writes nothing; it says whether the file is what the export makes.

## What's new data

`FlatUI/WhatsNewData.lua` holds the newest six versions of `CHANGELOG.md` for the **What's new** window, each cut after 40 lines with a last line that says how many more are in the full changelog. `tools/dev/make_whatsnew.py` makes it from `CHANGELOG.md`; run it after each change to the changelog:

```
python3 tools/dev/make_whatsnew.py
```

It needs Python 3. `--check` writes nothing; it says whether the file is what `CHANGELOG.md` makes.
