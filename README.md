# Flat UI

Flat UI restyles the default interface in WoW: Forever with flat backgrounds and softly shaded bar fills, borderless by default or with thin dark borders. It has its own options window and profiles. It has handles to move and size frames and windows. It also draws combat numbers in front of enemy nameplates and in scroll areas beside your character. In your bags and bank it marks items that bind when equipped, tints items you cannot use red and marks the food a hunter's pet eats.

Every setting starts at the settings of **Jon Default**, the author's own profile, and a new install and every new profile start with its frame positions, fitted to your screen at the scale the profile gives (a profile you have keeps its own).

A new character is welcomed with a picture of their own frame and a short setup that walks round the screen: your frame, your target and party, action bars, nameplates, chat and helpers, each with its choices drawn side by side. Nothing changes until you finish, and every setting stays in the options window.

The options window lists its pages in eleven groups, by what they set: **Look**, **Frames and bars**, **Action bars**, **Combat** (your own fight), **Group**, **Character and gear** (**Gear comparison**, **Spell tooltips** and **Hit table**), **HUD** (**Minimap**, **Buffs and debuffs**, **Quests and loot**, **Messages and timers**, **Experience and menu bars** and **Data panels**), **Chat and windows**, **Bags and items**, **Helpers** (**Errands**, **Drink and health macros**, **Mount key** and **World and cursor**) and **Layout**. A page that holds part of a module, such as **Buffs and debuffs** of Minimap and HUD, shows that module's switch.

Most settings apply at once. When a change needs a reload, wherever you made it, a small notice at the top of the screen says what needs it, with **Reload** (which also works where the game blocks reloads for add-ons, outside a fight) and **Later**, which hides it until your next such change; it goes by itself when you put the change back. Move it with the **Reload notice** box in `/flatui move`.

The **Errands** page, on by default, does the chores the game leaves to you: it sells grey items at merchants, and your sell list twelve at a time so the buyback still holds them, repairs with your gold or the guild's where you choose, buys a full stack or as many as your gold, the stock and your bags allow after a Shift-click and a question, can answer the merchant's questions before a purchase with tokens or the sale of tradeable loot, loots every slot at once, makes room in full bags, learns everything at a trainer with one button, guards Release Spirit in dungeons, types DELETE for you, and can accept summons, resurrections and invites from friends or your guild, decline duels and keep the last mail recipient. The Release Spirit guard, DELETE, summons, resurrections, invites, duels and the mail recipient are on its **Popups and invites** tab. Its **Quests and levelling** tab accepts and hands in quests, picks gossip options, offers the new ranks of your spells for your action bars, shows the reputation you gain, reminds you of talent points, adds /way and turns on the game's in-game navigation, its marker over the quest or map pin you track, which Forever's options leave out.

The **Data panels** page, on by default with its two panels off until you show them, adds a top and a bottom panel with bag space, gold across your characters, durability, your hearthstone, coordinates, frame rate and latency, friends and guild online, and the equipment set you wear, which a click changes. Each panel can be wider than its slots and sit over the game's windows, its text can take an outline, a shadow and your class colour or your own, and it can fade in or out of combat, back to full under the mouse. Frame rate and latency on the panels and by the minimap turn yellow and red when poor, and Flat UI's reputation bar can show reputation per hour and the time to the next standing.

The **Threat list** page, on by default, lists your group by threat on the enemy you fight, highest first, in class colours; at 100% a member has aggro. The **Healer mana** page, on by default, shows your healers' mana in dungeons and raids.

The **Bags** page, on by default, puts all your bags in one Flat UI window, in sections (new items, equipment sets, equipment, consumables, quest items, trade goods, reagents, recipes, ammunition, bags and quivers, keys, other, junk and free slots) or bag by bag, with a search that dims what does not match, your gold and your free slots. Nothing moves while the window is open: what you loot gathers at the top under New items, and anything else that comes into your bags meanwhile under Added. Drop an item on a section's title to keep it there, or on Junk, which is always there to drop on, to sell it at merchants from then on. Drag a section's title to put the sections in your own order, or right-click it to move it up or down. Ctrl+right-click an item to pin its slot to it (or drop it on Keep in place, if Ctrl is your Auto Loot Key): it shows in Keep in place, in the order of your bags and never sorted, and Flat UI puts it back in its slot after a clean up and when it comes back to your bags. Loot the game puts in an empty pinned slot while the window is open shows in that slot's place in Keep in place until the window opens again. A Flat UI bank window works at the banker, with Deposit and Take out for what you searched, and shows your bank anywhere else as you last left it. Pick another of your characters in a window's title to see their bags and bank, and an item's tooltip names who else on your realms carries it. A Sell junk button at merchants sells what Errands would sell, also with Errands off. The game's bag windows stay hidden while it is on; it stays off while another bag addon is loaded.

The **Drink and health macros** page, on by default, keeps two macros up to date with your bags and level: one drinks your best drink, conjured water first when it is as good, and the other takes a healthstone, then a healing potion, and eats out of combat.

The **Mount key** page, under **Helpers**, off by default, gives you one key in the game's Key Bindings that mounts a random mount you can ride, favourites first; a druid takes Aquatic or Travel Form and a shaman Ghost Wolf where a mount cannot go, and pressed again it dismounts or leaves the form. With **Slow Fall or Levitate while falling** on, a mage or priest who presses it while falling, out of a fight and not mounted, casts that spell on themselves instead.

The **Trackers** can count who in your group lacks your class buff (2/5 on its icon), and out of combat a click casts it on them; it also casts your own buff, stance or form, and calls, revives or sets your pet to fight. **Click to apply** can also put poisons, stones or oils on your weapon, or cast a shaman's weapon imbue. A pet tracker shows your pet's health in red while it is low. Trackers can warn minutes ahead of a long buff or poison, earlier in dungeons and raids, and play a sound when a buff comes back or gains a stack. The large proc art of **Proc alerts** has a **Proc art** box in `/flatui move` to put it anywhere, in the middle of the screen until you move it; **Art height** still raises or lowers it from there.

The **Group tools** page, on by default, adds a row of buttons for your group: the eight raid markers and a clear for your target, a ready check, a role poll and a pull countdown.

The **Ping bar** page, under **Group**, on by default, adds a bar with a button for each ping of the game's ping wheel, with the game's own icons: **Ping** (the game picks the kind), **Attack**, **Warning**, **On My Way** and **Assist**. Click a button, then click anywhere on the screen to ping that spot for your group, in a fight too: the cursor turns into the game's ping cursor and a line by the bar names the ping that waits; right-click or Esc cancels. Shift-click pings your target at once (with no target it works as a click). Each ping also has a key of its own under **Flat UI** in the game's Key Bindings (Not a threat and Threat too), which pings what is under the mouse at once, as the game's own ping keys do: hover the spot, press the key. Each button's tooltip says what a click and a Shift-click do and names its keys. It shows in a group, always or never (keys only), hides while pings are off in the game's options or your group lets only some members ping, and shows a swipe while the game makes you wait before the next ping. `/flatui move` places it; **Button size**, **Spacing**, **Layout** (horizontal or vertical) and **Borders in the ping's colour** set its look.

The **Absorb shields** page, under **Combat**, on by default, shows the shields on you as bars, in the manner of the NanShield display: the total of every absorb on you, and a bar for each Power Word: Shield, Ice Barrier, Mana Shield, Fire, Frost or Shadow Ward, Sacrifice, protection potion or item with its icon, the amount it has left, its time left and the colour of the school it takes. In a fight, while the game hides your buffs, the total keeps its live amount and each shield shows its time. A sound can play when a shield breaks, and your target's total can have a bar too.

Each unit frame shows its portrait beside its name row and bars, the portrait as tall as the frame, with the portrait and each bar in a thin border of its own. The portrait can be a face, square or round, a 3D head, a class icon or none, and **One frame**, under **Frame style** on the **All frames: bars and pictures** tab of **Unit frames**, puts the whole frame on one background inside one border, its parts the same few pixels apart.

On party and raid frames, a debuff you can dispel colours the border of the health bar, also on your own, your target's and your focus's frames, with its type icon on party frames. Party and raid frames show raid markers, raid frames the leader and assistants; a tank can keep a plain border while holding aggro, role icons can be limited to the roles you choose, and your target's border can take a colour of your own. With **Squares for your heals over time** (off by default), each heal over time of yours on a member, such as Renew, Rejuvenation or Regrowth, shows as a small square of its own colour in a corner of the health bar.

A druid in Bear or Cat Form sees their mana in a thin bar under the player frame. **Pet target frame**, off by default on the **Pet frame** tab of **Unit frames**, shows what your pet attacks, with its health and name; a click targets it.

The **Cooldowns** icons turn a spell red while your target is out of its range and blue while you lack the power for it, can show your healing potions, mana potions and healthstones with how many you carry, any item with a use, the on-use gear you wear and how many reagents you carry for a spell, can take the spells of an action bar at once, show tooltips, and can give each icon a ready sound of its own. Up to three more rows, each off until you switch it on, take the icons you give them with **Row of one icon**, with a place, size and direction of their own; each row can show only in combat, out of it, in a group, in a dungeon or raid or when not mounted, and can hang under the player or target frame. One icon can show only on cooldown, only when ready or with a border while it cools down, and icons can glow when ready or on procs and show the time left of the buff they gave you. Their **Cooldown pulse** tab, on by default, fades the icon of a spell, trinket or item you used in the middle of the screen when its cooldown ends, with a sound of its own for each spell or item if you like, at up to three spots, each spell or item at the spot you give it.

Nameplates show your threat on each enemy, on by default: the health bar shows threat you want, changing hands or unwanted, reading you as a tank from your group role, stance or form, and an enemy another tank or your pet holds has a colour of its own. On the **Threat** tab of **Nameplates**, a ring in the threat colour marks each enemy that needs you, enemies whose threat you want fade so the others stand out while your target and focus stay clear, and each enemy shows your share of its threat left of its health bar in a group or with a pet. Off by default, the health bar border and the name can take the threat colour, and the name of who an enemy attacks can show right of the bar. A tank and a damage dealer or healer can also have colours and fade opacities of their own, threat changing hands can fade, raid-marked enemies can stay clear, the health bar background and the health text can take the threat colour, and the threat value can sit right of, above or below the bar with its own offsets, outline and colour.

A warrior who knows **Overpower** sees its icon over the nameplate of an enemy that dodges them, on by default (**Overpower on enemy nameplates**, on the **Marks and icons** tab of **Nameplates**, with **Overpower icon size**). It glows while you can use Overpower now and is grey and still while you cannot (the wrong stance, too little rage, its cooldown), and it goes when you cast Overpower, after 5 seconds, or when the enemy dies or its nameplate goes. The game does not say whose attack was dodged, so a dodge counts as yours when that enemy is your target and you are in melee with it, or when Overpower turns usable at once after it.

Enemy cast bars on your target, focus and nameplates show whether your class interrupt is ready, and its icon on the cooldown row gets a gold border while your target or focus casts something you can interrupt.

**No target warning** and **Entering and leaving combat** (both on by default), on the **Combat text** page, show **No target** while you fight with no target or a dead or friendly one, and **+Combat** and **-Combat** as a fight starts and ends.

In the chat windows, a web address becomes a link that opens a box to copy it, and an item, spell or achievement link shows its tooltip while the mouse is over it. The **Tooltips** page names what a unit targets, colours a player's class line and puts icons before item and spell names; with IDs on it shows the ID of the poison, oil or stone on your weapon, and **Show IDs** can wait for Shift, Ctrl or Alt. The items you wear show beside an item's tooltip only while you hold Shift. With **Show recent chat after a reload** the chat of the last hour comes back in your main window after a reload. Tooltips can hide by kind (spells, items, and your buffs and debuffs and those on party and raid frames), unit tooltips by enemy or friend and only in dungeons and raids, and the unit tooltips of party and raid frames; **Icon ID** adds the icon's number.

Spell tooltips show what share of your spell damage, spell healing or attack power a spell or ability adds, a cast, a tick or a target, and with your stats how much that is now ("81.4% of Frost spell damage +417", **Spell tooltips** page, under **Character and gear**). Where the game's own text already counts your stats, as Revenge's does, the line says so. Weapon abilities show their weapon damage and the share of attack power their weapon's speed gives them, and a rank far below your level shows the lower spell power WoW: Forever gives it, by an estimate. A line names the threat of abilities with threat of their own and of spells your stance, form, Righteous Fury, talents or gear change, with each multiplier: Sunder Armor in Defensive Stance with Defiance 3/3 reads 552 with 1,000 attack power, and under it 206 + 5% of attack power; Defensive x1.8, Defiance 3/3 x1.2. The values are WoW: Forever's own, from the game's spell data and tooltips and, where they do not say, from tests in the game and the other sources named under Credits; ~ marks an estimate. In a fight the game withholds your stats, so the numbers are those from before it, while stances and forms count at once. While the game compares items (Shift), and at once in the tooltip of an item link you click in chat, an item you could wear shows how it changes the damage, healing or threat of the spells you chose against what you wear in its slot, and on the Character window what each item gives; by default your class's main spells for your role. The **Gear comparison** window (`/flatui gear`) shows all of them, now and with an item you drop on it. Spell Piercing counts there: a damage spell of a school of magic rolled on the Spell line does the damage your piercing adds (the hit table's Resist below 0, at your own level), so piercing on an item raises it, and the piercing of one school, as Fire Piercing, counts for that school's spells alone; the share an enemy above you resists for its levels is left out, as a spell that also slows or roots pays for those with its higher miss instead. Piercing that no spell compared takes is named as not counted. These lines need **Restyle chat and tooltips** on. **Game look** turns it off: turn on **Restyle chat and tooltips** on the **Chat** page and untick the rows whose look you want as the game has it, such as **Chat window background** and **Tooltip border and background**.

With **Avoidance** (**For tanks** by default, on the **Gear comparison** page) an item's tooltip also shows how it changes an enemy's swing against you: how often it misses you, is dodged, parried or blocked, crits or crushes, and what your armor and block value take off, against the enemy **Against** names, as in "Dodged +0.80% (13.40%)" and "Critical hit -0.60% (1.20%)", with a dim note of the defense at which you are immune. The **Gear comparison** window gets an **Avoidance** view with the whole table now and with the item, and `/flatui status` says the table now. The rules of misses, crits and crushing blows are the game's own; what a boss takes off your dodge, parry and block, and one roll for the whole table, are Classic's rules, marked ~.

Spell tooltips with power lines also give the **Total** of a cast with your stats, base and bonus together, as in "Total 973 a cast (556 + 417)": over the whole duration for a spell over time, with a tick, the whole channel, a heal's healing and one hit with the weapon you wear for a weapon strike. A dim line under it gives what a cast does on average with crits, misses and, for a spell, the damage your Spell Piercing adds (a heal with crits), a second of cast time (the global cooldown for an instant) and a point of mana. The spell and item lines share one look: a gold header over a block of more than one line, the figure on the right in white, a change green when it is better and red when it is worse, ~ before an estimate, and one dim line of notes a block (**Notes under the lines**: **Compact**, or **Full** for every note).

An item's lines, and the window, open with one **Overall** line, as in "Overall Damage +2.4% · Threat +1.1% · Damage taken -2.1%": the change in what the spells you chose do in a minute with the item, your swings and shots for the whole minute and each other spell as often as a minute of [WoWSims](https://github.com/wowsims/classic)' rotation for your class uses it, and Heroic Strike, Cleave, Maul and Raptor Strike only what they add over the swing they take the place of, once a swing at most, each spell times its weight (1 unless you set another in the window, from 0 to 5); damage and healing as at **A cast** whatever the switch shows, threat as at **Threat**, for a tank or in the window while the switch says Threat; and where avoidance shows, **Damage taken**, what an enemy's swing does to you after misses, dodges, parries, blocks, crits, crushing blows and your armor, with the item against without it (**Enemy hit for block value** sets the hit your block value is measured against). The window names the spell on show that gains the most and the one that loses the most, in percent as their rows say (none under **Avoidance**). The rotation is what the WoWSims Classic simulator does in a three-minute boss fight with the defaults of your class and spec: Fury, Arms and Protection warriors, Retribution and Protection paladins, hunters (Marksmanship's run, which stands in for the other two trees), Combat rogues, Shadow priests, Elemental and Enhancement shamans, Fire and Frost mages, warlocks with most points in Demonology or Destruction, Balance druids and feral druids in Cat or Bear Form. The spec is your druid form, else the tank's spec while your role is tank, else your talent tree with the most points. Each spell you chose counts its own uses a minute there, whichever others you chose, and a spell the rotation never casts counts nothing; Heroic Strike and Maul count what they add over the swing on the share of your swings the rotation queues them on (81% for a Fury warrior, half for Arms, nearly all for a tank), so with the swings they replace, which your auto attack counts, they come out as in a fight, where a Fury warrior's Heroic Strike deals about as much as all the melee swings (34% of the damage against 28%). Healers, the other trees and a kind of figure the rotation has no spell of (healing) keep the even share of the minute's 40 global cooldowns. The window's **Overall** tooltip names the rotation it counts. Your auto attack, Auto Shot, wand and seals count a second of your swings or shots, in the item's lines and in the window at **A cast** too, so a slow weapon is never set against a fast one swing for swing. Every weapon counts at the full skill of its line for your level, with what your race and gear add, now and with the item alike: the comparison is between items, so a sword you have yet to train up is not marked down for it, and Sunder Armor, Revenge and Shield Slam change only through the hit, crit and stats the item gives, and the skill your race or gear gives its kind of weapon. Where your skill in a compared weapon is lower, a note says so, as in "Swords 230/275: until you raise it you miss more". The hit table under the player frame and on enemy tooltips, and the line under a spell's tooltip of what a cast does with crits and misses, still count your skill as it is; pointed at in the window, a weapon spell's row says so. Drag its bottom right corner to size it (the size is kept for each character; **Reset the window** puts it back), and set the size of its text with **Window text size**. Its second item box, **Against**, compares an item with another of the same slot instead of what you wear. **Find** opens the **item finder** beside it: search the game's armor and weapons, your bags and bank or your other characters by name (or a pasted link), slot, armor or weapon type, level and quality, by default near your level and only what you can wear, sorted by item level or by **Best for me**. Click a result to compare it, right-click to compare against it, Shift-click to link it and Ctrl-click to try it on. On the Character window, a **Compare gear** button on the title bar, left of the close button, opens and closes the window (**Button on the Character window**), and its words light up while the window is open; with an item on the cursor, or one dropped on the button, the window compares that item, and a right-click opens the finder. The button comes and goes with the Character window however it opens, and takes its size. Where the title bar is crowded, as a long name in the narrow Character window can make it, the words fold away and the gear icon stays. Its box in `/flatui move` (**Compare gear button**) shows while the Character window is open: drag it anywhere and the button keeps that place on the Character window, moving with it; a right-click on the box puts it back on the title bar. The finder hangs on the window's right, or on its left when the screen has no room on the right. Drag its title bar, or its box in `/flatui move`, to give it a place of its own, which your profile keeps as it keeps every moved frame; **Dock** on its title bar, a right-click on the title or a right-click on its box in `/flatui move` hangs it on the window again. Its box shows in `/flatui move` only while the finder is open, so a closed finder is never in the way. **Minimize** folds it to its title bar with the search box, which each character keeps, and its box in `/flatui move` gets that small too. Damage taken shows once in an item's tooltip: in the **Overall** line, or with **Overall change** off in the avoidance lines.

B opens your reagent bag with the rest of your bags, and closes them all again: WoW: Forever binds B to Toggle Backpack, which with **Combine bags** leaves the reagent bag shut, so Flat UI gives that key the game's own Open All Bags while **Backpack key opens every bag** is on (**Item slots**).

**Show thorns**, on by default in the **Character window** section of **Item slots**, puts the damage an attacker takes each time it strikes you in melee on the Character window, under the right column of slots, as one total a hit: "Thorns 45". It stays clear of the weapon slots beside it and is cut short with "..." rather than run under them. It counts the Thorns, Retribution Aura and Fire Shield on you, other buffs whose text deals damage to attackers, and the items you wear that inflict damage on an attacker when struck, an enchantment or a set bonus that holds included; a shield spike, which strikes only on a block, follows the total where it fits ("Thorns 45, +20-30 on block"). Point at the line for each source with its school, the spike and the total of each school. Buffs are read while the game shows them; in a fight the line keeps those read before it. Lightning Shield's charges are not counted.

The **Fading** page, on by default, fades bars and frames while nothing needs them, and can show the target and focus frames, your buffs, the objective tracker and the game's damage meter only in combat, in a group, in or outside an instance or when not mounted. Its **Minimap buttons** row fades other addons' minimap buttons with the game's. Its **Bag bar** row fades the backpack, the bag slots, the reagent bag and the keyring together, and the mouse over any of them shows them all, also while **Leave the game's action bars alone** is on; its **Micro menu** row does the same for the micro menu's buttons and the help ticket button. **Micro menu faded opacity** and **Bag bar faded opacity** give those two a faded opacity of their own in place of **Faded opacity**, down to 0%, where they are gone until the mouse is over them and still take clicks. Its **Bag windows** row fades your open bag and bank windows, Flat UI's or the game's: a window shows in full as it opens, under the mouse, while an item is on the cursor, a stack is being split or you type in its search box.

With the [Questie](https://github.com/Questie/Questie) addon, **Questie's tracker**, on by default on the **Quests and loot** page, under **HUD**, gives Questie's quest tracker the look of the objective tracker: Flat UI's background and thin border round it with **Tracker background**, a flat band with a thin border behind its heading and each zone heading with **Tracker headers**, **Heading size** on the zone headings, a thin border and a cropped icon on its quest item buttons with **Tracker item buttons**, **Opacity in combat**, and, while the **Fonts** page is on, its interface font, outline and shadow on every line. Questie's own font sizes stay, since it lays its lines out by them, and Questie draws no bars to restyle. Off, or without Questie, Questie keeps its own look; Questie's settings are never changed.

The **Bar block** page, off by default, lines action bars 3 and 4, action bar 2 and the main bar up in one block, in that order from the top, with one box in `/flatui move`, **Action bar block**, also while **Leave the game's action bars alone** is on, which then leaves only the bars in no block to the game. While the page is on, Flat UI's experience bar sits right under block 1, exactly as wide as the block, and follows it as its bars, their size, its direction or its box change; the block keeps its room, and the stance and pet bars stay above it (**Experience bar under the bar block**, on by default on the **Experience and menu bars** page). With that row off, or with the page off as it is by default, the experience bar has its own box in `/flatui move`, at the place a new profile starts with until you move it, and its own **Width** again. **One size and spacing for every bar**, on by default, gives the bars of the blocks one button size and row length and lays action bars 4 and 5 flat like the others, after a reload. Up to three blocks each have their own bars, in the order you set with **Bars** (move a bar up or down, or take it out) and **Add a bar** (the main bar and bars 2 to 8, each in one block only), or in `/flatui move`, where each bar of a block has a tab inside the block's box: drag the tab up or down and let go, and the bar takes that place in its block, as a line across the block shows while you drag; drop it on another block's box to put it in that block. Escape, or letting go outside every box, leaves the bars as they were. The two ways change the same order. Each block has its own stack direction, alignment and gap, and its own box in `/flatui move`; blocks 2 and 3 are off until you give them a bar, and start beside block 1. A bar of a block that the game's options have off is turned on there, at login and when you add it. **Fade the block** eases the bars down to **Faded opacity** over **Fade time** (300 ms by default) once the mouse has been off the block for **Wait before fading** (500 ms), so a mouse passing over them on its way across the screen makes them flicker no more, and brings them back to full at once; with Flat UI's own action buttons it fades those, and the game's bars hidden under them stay hidden. The **Class and item bars** page hangs the Class spells, Buffs to apply and Consumables bars from block 1.

The **Hidden frames** page (on, with nothing ticked) takes frames of the game off the screen: tick one and it is gone, untick it and it is back as it was. It lists the game's HUD frames that `/flatui move` moves, in groups (action bars, unit frames, minimap, buffs and debuffs, bars and timers, messages and alerts, trackers and meters), and the parts of the minimap one by one: the zone name, the tracking, mail, calendar, clock, addon and zoom buttons. The chat, the loot window, the tooltip, the windows (Character, Spellbook, bags and the rest) and Flat UI's own frames are not on it. The pet frame, the totems and the class resource bars go with the player frame while Edit Mode keeps them under it, and their boxes in `/flatui move` say so. **Alerts** takes each toast away as it comes. A hidden frame stays hidden when the game shows it again, also after a reload, and each tick's tip says what goes with it and what still works, as the keys of a hidden action bar, or B and the bag keys without the bag bar. In `/flatui move`, Shift and right-click a box to hide its frame (on a box with no tick it only says so in chat), or press **Hide frame** in the inspector; the **Hidden** group at the top of the frame list shows a frame again with its eye. A change in a fight waits for its end. While **Leave the game's action bars alone** is on, the game's bars stay; the micro menu and the bag bar hide either way, and the keys such as C, P and L still open their windows. The main bar and the frames the game stacks above it, such as the cast bar, keep their places with the micro menu hidden. `/flatui move` still does not move the micro menu while that row is on, since the game hangs its main bar from it. **Fading** leaves a hidden frame alone: it comes back at its own opacity and fades again from there. `/flatui hide` opens the page.

Loot rolls get Flat UI's look with **Loot rolls**, on by default on the **Quests and loot** page: a flat background and thin border, the item's icon cropped with a border in its quality colour, the name in that colour, a flat timer bar with a thin border, and **Need**, **Greed** and **Pass** in a row; off gives the game's look back. Move them with the **Loot rolls** box in `/flatui move`: until you do, the game places them, and once you have, they stack from there, up or down as **Rolls stack** says. Right-click the box to give them back to the game.

**Hide error messages**, on by default on the **Messages and timers** page, hides the kinds of red error messages you choose in the middle of the screen, with their sound and voice: not enough mana, rage or energy, out of range, facing the wrong way, not ready yet, no or an invalid target, and not while moving. **Hide the zone title**, **Hide the subzone title** and **Hide the talking head**, off by default on the same page, take away the big zone name and the talking head with its voice. **Clear the damage meter**, off by default on the same page, clears the game's damage meter as you enter a dungeon or raid, or asks first.

**Hit table under the player frame**, on the **Hit table** page, under **Character and gear**, shows under your player frame your chances against your target to miss, be dodged, parried or blocked, glance, crit and hit, worked out again as your gear, buffs, weapon skill, form and target change: a line for your swings (each hand while you dual wield), your special attacks, your ranged attacks and your spells, picked by your class and form, with the crit cap of your swings, yellow once your crit is past it; a target whose level shows as ?? counts as three levels above you. Your spells also get **Resist**, no part of the roll: the share of a spell's damage resisted on average, with your Spell Piercing. The enemy's own resistances are not known and count as none, so with piercing it goes below 0, in green, for the damage a creature takes more (WoW: Forever's Spell Vulnerability, half of what a point of resistance takes, a point below 0; against a player piercing does nothing below 0); and a creature above you resists about 2% more a level, Classic's rule, an estimate on WoW: Forever that piercing does not lower, which a spell that also slows or roots pays with its higher miss instead. The game gives only the piercing of every school, so piercing of one school, as Fire Piercing, counts in the gear comparison and a spell's Expected line and not here; `/flatui status` says what Resist counts. By default it is **For tanks and damage dealers**: it shows while you count as a tank or a damage dealer and hides while you count as a healer, reading your role as Flat UI setup does (your group role, else a tank stance, form or aura, else the talent tree you put most points in, else your class: a priest heals), and follows a new group role, your talents, a switch of dual spec, your level and a change of form or aura; `/flatui status` says which role you count as and why. **Always** and **Off** hold whatever your role. **On enemy tooltips**, on the same page and also **For tanks and damage dealers** by default, adds your hit table to the tooltip of an enemy you can attack: a line for each attack, as in "Swing  M8 D6.5 G40 C12.1 H33.4" (M miss, D dodge, P parry, G glance, B block, C crit, H hit, in percent; R on the Spells line, its Resist, as in R-2.5, with a dim note of what it counts while it is not 0), against that enemy's level (a level shown as ??, or a boss of your level or above, counts as three levels above you), with parries and blocks only from the front (a tank counts in front), following **Attacks**, **Special attacks**, **Where you stand** and **Hit cap against a boss** whatever **Hit table under the player frame** is set to; those rows stay open while it is **Off**. In a fight the game hides your stats, so the numbers there are from before it, and a dim line says so. With **Only while Ctrl is held**, under it and on by default, the lines show only with Ctrl: held as the tooltip shows, or pressing Ctrl adds the lines at once; they leave when the tooltip next changes, not as you let go (over a unit frame, whose tooltip the game sets again every 0.2 seconds, they go with the key). The tooltip is never built again for them, so it is otherwise as the game has it; **Hint while Ctrl is up** (off) puts one dim line, "Hold Ctrl for the hit table", in their place meanwhile, which becomes their header as you press Ctrl. It needs **Chat and tooltips** on. **Text size**, a dropdown on the same page, sets the size of the table's text, from 70% to 200%; a pick applies at once and is kept with your profile. **Text size on enemy tooltips** does the same for the hit table's lines on enemy tooltips alone: the tooltip's other lines, and every tooltip after it, keep the game's size.

The **World and cursor** page, under **Helpers**, can let the camera zoom out farther than the game's own options allow, and draw a ring around the mouse pointer, always or in combat only, with your cast running round it and, if you like, the global cooldown. The unit frames can hide the hit numbers the game flashes over your portrait and your pet's.

**Lava tick timer**, on the same page and **In lava zones** by default, helps you walk or swim through lava. Lava burns you every 2 seconds, and a jump timed so you are in the air as the tick lands takes no damage, even when you land back in it. After a fire hit on you in Molten Core, Blackrock Depths or Spire, Ragefire Chasm, Searing Gorge, Burning Steppes or Un'Goro Crater (or anywhere, with **Anywhere**), a bar under your character counts down to the next tick and turns green with **Jump** for the last **Jump lead** (400 ms by default), with a flash and, if you pick one, a **Jump sound**. Each tick says **Hit**, and a tick you were in the air for says **Dodged**; the bar goes 5 seconds after the last one. It is an estimate: the game tells add-ons that fire hit you, not that it was lava, so in a fight it counts only fire hits that keep the 2-second rhythm, and the first tick of each visit always lands. `/flatui move` places it, `/flatui lava` says what it sees, `/flatui lava test` shows a sample and `/flatui lava trace` prints each hit on you. The idea is the timing bar of the Classic WeakAura "Avoid lava damage"; none of its code is used.

## Install

1. Download the newest file from [CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/projects/1750813) or from the releases of this repository.
2. Put the `FlatUI` folder in the `Interface\AddOns` folder of your WoW: Forever install.
3. Start the game and type `/flatui`.

## Commands

- `/flatui` opens the settings window.
- `/flatui setup` walks round your screen in six short stops and saves what you pick.
- `/flatui profiles` shows the ready-made looks. A click puts one in use.
- `/flatui move` shows a box for every frame. Drag a box to move its frame; Shift and right-click the box of a HUD frame of the game to hide it.
- `/flatui hide` opens the Hidden frames page, where a tick hides a frame of the game.
- `/flatui status` says what is styled, and any error.
- `/flatui sell <item>` always sells an item at merchants (an item link or ID). Type it again to stop. `/flatui sell` alone lists the items. **Items always sold** on the Errands page takes one off.
- `/flatui keep <item>` never sells a grey item, and Make room never deletes it. **Items never sold** takes one off.
- `/flatui train` learns everything you can pay for at an open trainer, like the Train all button.
- `/flatui npc` skips the NPC you talk to or target: its quests and gossip are left to you. Type it again to stop.
- `/flatui gossip` lists the options of the NPC you talk to. `/flatui gossip 2` picks the second option for you from now on.
- `/flatui uprank` offers the new ranks of your spells for your action bars.
- `/flatui rank <spell>` keeps that rank where it is on your bars (a spell link or ID).
- `/way 45 61` puts a pin on your map and the game points you to it; `/way Elwynn Forest 45 61` for another zone, `/way clear` to take it off. `/flatui way` does the same when another addon has `/way`.
- `/flatui xp` says your experience per hour, time to level and kills and quests to level, and your reputation per hour and time to the next standing with the faction on Flat UI's bar. `/flatui xp reset` starts counting again.
- `/flatui threat` shows sample bars of the threat list for a few seconds, out of combat; `/flatui healers` does the same for the healer mana list.
- `/flatui lava` says what the lava tick timer sees: the place and its IDs, the ticks seen and dodged, the last hit on you and the gaps between ticks. `/flatui lava test` shows a sample for 8 seconds, and `/flatui lava trace` prints a line for each hit on you, to check in lava that the game reports its ticks.
- `/flatui cooldown add healthstone` puts your healthstones on the cooldown row, every rank as one icon; `healing` and `mana` do the same for potions, and `/flatui cooldown remove` takes one off.
- `/flatui cooldown sound <spell> bell` gives one icon of the cooldown row its own ready sound; `row` gives it back the row's.
- `/flatui cooldown add item <item>` puts an item with a use, such as the Hearthstone, on the cooldown row (an ID, a link or a name); `remove item` takes it off. `/flatui cooldown import 1` adds the spells with a cooldown on action bar 1 (2 to 8 likewise).
- `/flatui pulse test` shows a cooldown pulse; `/flatui pulse ignore <spell or item>` leaves one out and `/flatui pulse watch` puts it back; `/flatui pulse list` says what pulses.
- `/flatui pulse sound <spell or item> bell` gives one its own pulse sound; `none` silences it, and `page` gives it the page's sound again.
- `/flatui pulse spot <spell or item> 2` sends one to pulse spot 2; `1` puts it back.
- `/flatui role` says whether nameplate threat reads you as a tank and why; `tank`, `damage`, `auto` or `toggle` sets it.
- `/flatui gear` opens or closes the Gear comparison window; `/flatui gear <item>` compares a shift-clicked item there. The window's **Find** button opens the item finder. `/flatui gear add <spell>` (an ID, a name or a shift-clicked spell, which keeps its rank), `remove <spell>`, `bar 1` and `reset` change the spells it compares, `weight <spell> <0 to 5>` sets how much a spell counts in the **Overall** line, and `list` prints them.
- `/flatui tracker add group` tracks your class buff on your group; `/flatui tracker use 2 <item>` sets what weapon tracker 2 applies; `/flatui tracker set 1 health 40` shows pet tracker 1's health in red under 40%.
- `/flatui bags` opens or closes the Flat UI bag window; `/flatui bags <name>` shows another of your characters' bags.
- `/flatui bank` opens or closes your bank window: live at the banker with Flat UI's bank window, else as you last left it; `/flatui bank <name>` shows another character's bank.
- `/flatui test` turns test mode on or off (`/flatui test on` and `/flatui test off` also work); see **Test mode** below.
- `/flatui test icons` shows three sample icons for 8 seconds on Defensives on you, Your buffs, Party cooldowns and Enemy cooldowns.
- `/flatui help` lists the commands.

## Test mode

Test mode shows your frames on screen with sample content while you change options, so a setting can be seen as you change it, the frames that are hidden until a fight, a target or a group included. Turn it on with the **Test mode** switch on the title bar of the options window, left of Close; **Samples** on the toolbar of `/flatui move`; `/flatui test`; or a key (Key Bindings > Flat UI > **Flat UI test mode**). It does not turn on in a fight or while Setup has the options window.

A bar at the top of the screen (under the `/flatui move` toolbar while that shows, and just above or under the options window where it would cover that window's title bar; drag it to put it elsewhere until a reload) has **Group** (**Solo**, **Party**, **Raid 10**, **Raid 20** or **Raid 40**; Party at first, kept until a reload), **Moving** (on; off holds every sample still so a look can be judged), **Bags**, **Bank**, **Gear** and **Finder**, which open Flat UI's own windows with their real content, and **End**. A dim line under it names any frame that has no place yet ("open Edit Mode once"): a frame the game has never placed gets no sample at a corner.

What shows, each at its saved place, scale and size, drawn with your current settings:

- **Party frames** (Group Party, while you are in no group): Mara, a paladin tank and leader with a marker and aggro; Eli, a priest healer with a debuff you can dispel, your heals over time and out of range, whose health drifts; Ivo, a warlock with a Voidwalker; and Fay, a dead rogue. With Edit Mode's Raid-Style Party Frames they are cells, you first.
- **Raid frames** (Raid 10, 20 or 40, while you are in no raid and the game shows no party frames): that many members in groups, at the cell size and group layout of the Edit Mode layout in use, with every class and role, dead, offline and out of range members, debuffs you can dispel, markers on the tanks, a leader and two assistants, buffs and heals over time, as the game's raid frame options say. Three members' health drifts.
- **Target**, **target of target** (with the game's Target of Target on), **focus**, **focus target**, **pet** and **boss** frames 1 to 5 while there is none, and a shaman's **totems**, whose time counts down.
- **Cast bars**: yours fills, channels and is interrupted in turn; the target's, focus's, pet's and first boss's run while their sample frame shows. **Swing timers** while the game would show them (its Enable Swing Timer on, their Edit Mode Visibility not Hidden, the off hand and ranged ones with such a weapon), **debuffs** and **buffs** while you have none and their Edit Mode Visibility shows them, two **loot rolls** at the Loot rolls box, and a **sample nameplate** in the middle of the screen with its cast and threat colours.
- Flat UI's own frames: **combat text** and **combat alerts**, the **cooldown pulse**, **cooldown icons** (every row on, with sweeps that loop; an empty row gets three samples, one icon stays ready, and your interrupt is ringed in gold), **Defensives on you** and **Your buffs**, **Absorb shields** (a Power Word: Shield and a Fire Ward whose times loop, their total, and your target's total while its row is on), **party cooldowns** (on your real rows, or with no group sample rows for you and the party above), **enemy cooldowns** on the target and focus bars, **trackers** stepping fine, low and missing whatever their Show rule, your class's first **proc alert** with its art and glow, the **threat list**, **healer mana**, the **hit table** (against a boss with no target), the sample text of empty **data panel** slots, **group tools**, the **ping bar** (also while pings are blocked, greyed, with one sample cooldown swipe), the **cursor ring** and the **lava tick timer** (a tick every 2 seconds, Hit and Dodged in turn).

Every change shows at once; a frame moved or scaled in `/flatui move` or Edit Mode takes its sample with it within a quarter of a second. A sample steps aside as soon as the real frame shows: a real target, cast, group or aura. Esc > Edit Mode still shows the game's own previews, and the samples give way to them. A frame ticked on the **Hidden frames** page gets no sample, and neither does a frame that hangs from it (the pet frame, the totems and a cast bar locked to the player frame).

It ends when you press the switch, **Samples**, `/flatui test`, the key or **End** again; when you close the options window it was turned on from (not while `/flatui move` holds that window); when `/flatui move` closes for good after turning it on (unless the options window is open, which then keeps it); when Setup takes the options window; and at a reload. A fight pauses it, and it comes back after the fight while what turned it on is still open. It saves nothing: no setting, game setting, key binding, target or focus, and no number counts in a statistic.

What does not show, and why:

- **The game's own unit frames are never shown or given a unit**, and **Edit Mode is never opened by Flat UI**: the game protects them, and doing either from an addon taints them and can block targeting. The samples are Flat UI's own frames drawn where the game's are, so a sample can differ slightly from the real frame.
- **Real nameplates are not forced on screen** (that would change game settings), so threat colours show on the sample plate, not on your target's plate.
- **The game's windows** (Character, Spellbook, talents and the rest) are not opened: an addon opening them can taint the game's window manager. Open them yourself and the **Windows** page restyles them as you change it.
- **No drawing exists** for mirror timers, the loot window, the queue panel, alerts, zone text, the talking head, the extra action button, arena and vehicle frames, and Errands' overlays need the NPC's window open. Chat and tooltips get no sample: a printed line would skip the chat filters, and a tooltip shows on any mouse-over anyway.
- **Party cooldown rows hang in the raid stack**, not beside the party samples, and the **pet's target** frame (a secure button of Flat UI's) shows no sample.

## Test

`FlatUI_test.lua` runs the addon in a simulated client. It needs Lua 5.4.

```
lua5.4 FlatUI_test.lua FlatUI
```

The last line must say `N passed, 0 failed`.

## Code map

`tools/dev/flatui_map.py` builds the code map of Flat UI as one page: each file with its functions, its settings, its calls to other files, and the game's frames, functions, events and CVars it uses. It needs Python 3, Lua 5.4 and git.

```
python3 tools/dev/flatui_map.py --fetch
```

`--fetch` clones Forever's interface source and the client lists of [wowless](https://github.com/wowless/wowless), which name the game's functions, into `tools/dev/sources`, or brings them up to date. The page is `tools/dev/out/flat-ui-map.html`, with its data beside it.

## Spell data

`FlatUI/SpellData.lua` holds the spell values of the spell lines and the gear comparison. `tools/dev/make_spelltips.py` makes it from the inputs in `tools/dev/spelltips`. After a client build or a hotfix, read the client's spell rows again from a new capture of wowsims/forever (`assets/db_inputs/spell_store_inputs.json`, with GrunzForever's export for the few rows it lacks), then make the file:

```
python3 tools/dev/spelltips_import.py spell_store_inputs.json.gz wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite
python3 tools/dev/make_spelltips.py
```

It needs Python 3. `make_spelltips.py --check` writes nothing; it lists every value of the inputs the new rows contradict.

## Rotation data

`FlatUI/RotationData.lua` holds the WoWSims rotations the gear comparison's **Overall** line counts: each ability's uses a minute and its share of the damage and threat, for each class and spec, and the share of the main hand's swings Heroic Strike and Maul take. `tools/dev/make_rotations.py` makes it from `tools/dev/rotations/data.json`, the results of the WoWSims Classic simulator's command line run with each spec's presets, and `FlatUI/SpellData.lua`, whose keys it matches the abilities to:

```
python3 tools/dev/make_rotations.py
```

It needs Python 3. `--check` writes nothing; it says whether the file is what the data makes.

## Item index

`FlatUI/ItemIndex.lua` holds the armor and weapons the item finder searches: each item's ID, English name, class and subclass, required level, item level, quality and the classes that may wear it, one string a slot, read only when the finder first searches that slot. `tools/dev/make_itemindex.py` makes it from GrunzForever's export of the client tables (`wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite`), leaving out the client's test and placeholder items:

```
python3 tools/dev/make_itemindex.py wow_wowf_1.60.1.70888.sqlite
```

It needs Python 3. `--check` writes nothing; it says whether the file is what the export makes.
